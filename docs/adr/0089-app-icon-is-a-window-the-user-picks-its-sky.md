# Pane's icon is a window whose sky the user picks, and the bundle carries it in Chromium's place, dated so macOS cannot show Chromium's

Pane's icon is a real window: a white enamel frame, two panes, and a mullion, with the sky behind the glass. It was rendered in Magnific with ten skies, and the user kept all ten and asked that Pane's users pick one (Paper: 01.2). The ten are Day, a clear blue sky with no sun, Clouds, Morning, Dusk, Fuchsia, Sunset, Twilight, Afterglow, Night, and Moonrise. Fuchsia carries the colours of a drawn direction the user liked (01.5, P2), which its render took as reference. The skies are cartoon ones, at the user's request (2026-10-29): round, puffy shapes with soft light and volume, each redone in Magnific from its earlier render, keeping its colours and its elements in the same places, and approved one by one (Paper: 01.15). Night is the exception, the realistic sky with fewer, cleaner stars, the quietest of five options after the user's "less is more" (Paper: 01.16); Sunset, given the same options, went back to its first cartoon. Twilight has three stars at different heights and sizes, its first, centred at the top of its pane, being too even (Paper: 01.17).

The user also asked that the icon follow light and dark. Settings › Appearance › App icon offers Automatic, the default, then the ten. Automatic shows Day while the app's appearance is light and Night while it is dark: the theme sets that appearance (ADR 0001), and under system the Mac does.

The row shows the chosen icon alone. A click opens a dialog over the window, as Quit Pane? is drawn (Paper: 06.2): a `ModalDialog` (`pane/ui/panels/app_icon_picker.cc`), made once after Settings' first frame. It holds every sky as a tile, six a row: the icon at 56 points over its name, the chosen one ringed as the profile sheet's swatch is. The ring sits 2 to 4 points out from the icon's body, on the system's corner (continuous, 0.257 of the side, measured on macOS 27). A click applies a sky at once and the Dock follows, so the dialog stays open to compare. Done, Return, Esc, or a click outside closes it (ADR 0079). Choosing is instant, and hover fades (DESIGN.md).

Every sky sits in the one frame: Day's, whitened. The user asked for whiter frames of the same measurements, and the renders' frames differed in tone, thickness, and opening, from a few pixels to a lilac or golden cast. So each render gives only its glass. Day's frame keeps its shading and its colour neutral, and is close to white: a shade's distance from white, d, becomes d²/200, so the broad faces whiten most and the bevels and the lip by the glass keep their depth. The curve answers the user's request for a whiter frame still (2026-10-29), where a lift of 45% toward white stopped at a mean luminance of 229 of 255, against 241. Each sky's panes are cut 20 pixels in from their outer sides, past the rounded corners' frame, and 3 from the mullion, so what sits by it stays (Sunset's sun, Moonrise's moon). They are stretched into Day's panes.

The bundle's icon is an Icon Composer document, `packaging/icon/AppIcon.icon`: Day, with Night as its dark appearance. Under the Mac's dark icon style (macOS 26 and later), Finder, Launchpad, and the Dock while Pane is closed show Night. Each render becomes a full-bleed layer whose frame edge sits 12 points past the canvas, so the system's squircle mask cuts inside the frame. macOS 26 and 27 shrink an icon that does not fill their squircle onto a grey plate. While Pane runs, `pane/ui/app/dock_icon.cc` gives the Dock the chosen sky, at its tile's 128 points, and under Automatic it follows Pane's light or dark: AppKit had drawn the whole 1024 pixels twice at each change, 15.6 ms of the UI thread, where this takes 1.45. Those images, `pane/ui/panels/app_icons/AppIcon<Sky>.png`, are macOS's own drawing of an app whose icon is that sky's window alone, so the running icon matches the bundle's to the pixel; they are carried in the framework's Resources (`base::DIR_ASSETS`) and decoded off the UI thread (`app_icon_images.cc`). `packaging/icon/make-icons.swift` makes all of them from `packaging/icon/renders`. It also makes the README's logo, Day on a light page and Night on a dark one.

**On Windows the taskbar shows the same sky** (2026-11-13, commit `23bfc73`): the ten images are copied beside `chrome.dll` (`base::DIR_ASSETS` there, `pane/BUILD.gn`'s `app_icons`), where Settings' picker reads them too, and DockIcon's Windows half gives the chosen one to each of Pane's windows (`WindowHost::SetIcon`: `WidgetDelegate::SetIcon` and `SetAppIcon`, then `Widget::UpdateWindowIcon`, which Windows sizes for Alt+Tab and the title bar), and to a window opened later. Where Start's shortcut names Pane's AppUserModelID, as an install makes it, the taskbar's button shows that shortcut's icon, not the window's (a shortcut's rewrite, below), so the button follows the sky through the shortcut. Before, Windows' build carried none, so the taskbar showed Chromium's and Settings' picker showed no sky. They are macOS's drawing of the icon, its shadow and margin with it. **Automatic reads the system's dark or light on the turn after the theme's change** (2026-11-17, commit `f81ca5f`; `DockIcon::OnNativeThemeUpdated`): once, in `windows-install` in the test VM (2026-11-16, in the checklist run by hand on Windows), the Start shortcut, the taskbar's icon, and the window's icon stayed Night for minutes after Windows went light, the window itself light, until the next change of Windows' theme. The code shows no notification that comes before the scheme it reads (the system's provider updates it, then notifies); what it shows stuck that way is a sky's image that wouldn't read, which left that sky wanted with nothing shown, every later change finding it wanted already. A change's read now waits for its turn to end, several in a turn read once, and an image that won't read puts the wanted sky back to the one shown, so the next change asks again. `app-icon` turns Windows dark and light three times in the test VM alone, Pane's theme on System, and Automatic shows Night and Day each time. That account was not the cause, and the Night came back in 0.21.0's candidate run on Windows (a shortcut's rewrite, below): the read and the image were right; the shortcut's rewrite failed. The read on the turn after stays, harmless.

**A shortcut's rewrite that fails is tried again** (2026-11-17, commit `509811b`). The Night that stayed after Windows went light (in the checklist run by hand on Windows and in 0.21.0's candidate run, 2026-11-16 and 2026-11-17: 2 of 5 runs of `windows-install` in the test VM) was Start's `Pane.lnk`, and the taskbar's button with it; the window was light, and its own icon Day. Logged at each link in the test VM, starved as a loaded Mac starves it (QEMU paused and run in turns), 3 changes of 15 within seconds of a launch stuck so, each at the rewrite alone: `ERROR_USER_MAPPED_FILE` (1224), another process holding the shortcut mapped, as Windows holds it while it resolves a window's AppUserModelID to it for the taskbar's button (a save of the shortcut failed so as Pane's window appeared, in 2 launches of 10, and went through 20 to 200 ms later). `PointShortcutsAt` dropped the failure, and `DockIcon`, its sky shown, asked nothing again until the sky changed, which is how Windows' next change set it right. Chromium's registry watch heard every change, and the read, the image, and the window's icon were right each time. Now `PointShortcutsAt` counts the shortcuts it found and couldn't read or rewrite, and DockIcon's Windows half tries a failed rewrite again after 0.1 s, twice as long each time, eight times (25.5 s in all), a newer sky stopping the tries. The taskbar's button reads the shortcut when the window's icon changes and the shortcut's icon changes right after it (measured: the icon alone, the shortcut alone, the shortcut first, or the shortcut a second after the icon left the button as it was), so each try gives the windows their icon again first, as the change did. A test copy, whose launch left every shortcut alone so as not to touch the user's, now has Start's folder in its own home too (`TakeTestCopyHome`), with the desktop's and the pins' already there, and follows the shortcuts in it when all three lie in the temporary folder, so a scenario can read them. Since 2026-11-18 (commit `dde4090`) the home's first launch makes Start's `Pane.lnk` there as the install makes the user's (`MakeStartShortcut`, which `Register` calls too): Chrome's toasts follow that shortcut (ADR 0109), and `app-icon` reads it where it wrote a plain one of its own.

**Windows' icon files** (2026-11-15, commit `220a9c7`; Paper 11.12.1, 11.12.2, 11.12.15, 11.12.16, the user's calls C, D, and F). Where Windows reads a file rather than a window, it reads one of these, beside chrome.exe in the app's folder (`pane/BUILD.gn`'s `app_icons`, `packaging/windows/pane.release`), each made by `packaging/icon/make-icons.swift` from the ten images, with no new art:
- **`Pane.ico`**, Day, the sky the Mac's bundle carries: the Start menu's and the desktop's shortcuts, Installed apps, and Default apps name it. chrome.exe carries it too (patch 0001's `chrome_exe.rc`, ADR 0004), so Task Manager, the executable's properties, Explorer's view of the folder, and a launch refused before Pane's window show Pane's where they showed Chromium's.
- **`AppIcon-<Sky>.ico`**, one per sky: a shortcut that follows the App icon setting names its sky's file (D), a new path being what Windows' icon cache can't serve stale. Automatic names Day or Night with Pane's theme. The register names the data's sky at install (commit `6d66278`, ADR 0127), and from then `DockIcon` rewrites Start's and the desktop's shortcuts and each pin naming Pane's chrome.exe whenever the sky it shows changes and at launch, on the shell's COM thread at USER_VISIBLE, which Chromium never holds until its startup ends; another app's pin is left alone.
- **`PaneHTM.ico` and `PanePDF.ico`**, which Pane's ProgIDs name while Pane is the default for pages and PDFs (F, placement 1): a portrait page with its fold, the window under the fold, and on the PDF's the window raised over a "PDF" tag at the page's foot, a bar with no letters below 48; always Day, a document naming its type, not the window. Their geometry is the board's at each size, the page's edge and fold on whole pixels from 16 to 48, where the board's fractions would blur them, and a shadow keeping the bottom row. The tag's letters are the board's Inter Bold, which `make-icons.swift` registers from `packaging/icon/fonts/` (the SIL Open Font License beside it), since no Mac carries it, rather than the Mac's own bold, whose license is Apple's.

Each holds 16, 20, 24, 32, 40, 48, 64, 96, and 256 pixels, Windows taking the size a place asks for and drawing the next larger one down where it is missing; below 256 a 32-bit bitmap with its mask, the 256 a PNG, as Windows' own and Chromium's `IconUtil` write them. Each size is drawn down from the 1024 image the window shows with Lanczos3, the filter Views draws the window's own icon down with (`skia::ImageOperations::RESIZE_BEST`), so a file's icon and the window's are one image: at 16 the window keeps its margin and shadow, its body about 13 pixels, as the taskbar's does (11.11's B). `make-icons.swift windows` makes these alone from the images as they are, with no Xcode and the Mac's icons untouched.

The bundle carries this icon in Chromium's place. `scripts/package.sh` compiles `AppIcon.icon` with `actool` into a catalog of the icon alone (3 MB, its `Assets.car` and `AppIcon.icns`) and puts it in the app and in the two notification helpers, `Pane Helper (Alerts)` and `Pane Helper (Aperitif Alerts)`, whose icon notifications show: each loses Chromium's `app.icns`, and its `CFBundleIconFile` and `CFBundleIconName` name `AppIcon`. macOS caches an app's icon by its path and date, so `package.sh` gives every `.app` in the bundle the build's date and registers the app with LaunchServices (`lsregister -f`). The bundle names no document icon: the Swift build's asset catalog carried one, `Icon`, which no document type of the bundle named, 0.19.2's included, and it went with that catalog.

What was rejected:

- **Reading the scheme from Pane's own side** (the window's theme) rather than Chromium's provider, for the shortcut's Night: the provider was right each time; the shortcut's rewrite was what failed.
- **Retrying inside `PointShortcutsAt`, on the shell's thread:** a newer sky would wait behind an older one's tries, and each try needs the windows' icon given again first, which is the UI thread's.
- **Replacing the shortcut by a new file renamed over it:** untried against a mapped file, and a new file under the same name is another item to Start; trying again is enough.
- **A relaunch icon on the window** (`PKEY_AppUserModel_RelaunchIconResource`, Chrome's way to give a window its own taskbar icon): it didn't move the button here (2026-11-17), and pinning the running window would take it.
- **Changing the window's AppUserModelID and back,** which has the taskbar read the shortcut again: the button regroups, visibly.

- **An alternate icon API:** AppKit has none (iOS has `setAlternateIconName`).
- **A custom icon on the bundle** (`NSWorkspace.setIcon`): it writes into the signed app, and every update loses it.
- **The engine bundle's own dates:** `package.sh` copies it with `ditto`, which keeps them, so `Pane.app` kept the date it had when its icon was Chromium's (2026-10-26 04:12). Finder showed Pane's icon and LaunchServices had the right `AppIcon`, but the Dock kept Chromium's.
- **The app's catalog in the notification helpers:** with the Dock's skies, it would add 4.5 MB to each.
- **An asset catalog icon set with transparent corners:** macOS 26 and later jail it on the grey plate.
- **Runtime images exported with `ictool`:** they came out 2.8% brighter than the system's drawing of the same icon, a jump the Dock would show at launch.
- **A pop-up menu of the skies:** its thumbnails were too small to compare.
- **The tiles in the row itself:** the user found them heavy in Settings; they moved to the dialog.
- **Each render's own frame:** no two matched in tone or measure, and asking Magnific again matches none either.
- **More skies in Automatic:** the user asked for light and dark.
- **The skies the user turned down:** Day with the sun (Day lost it), a room reflected in a midday sky, a crescent moon with a blue glow on the frame, a moon behind clouds, a shooting star, and two oversized full moons.
- **A Dock tile plug-in for now** (`NSDockTilePlugIn`): it would keep the chosen sky in the Dock while Pane is closed. It waits for a Developer ID: on 2026-10-30 the Dock refused one in an ad-hoc signed copy ("Refusing to connect extra … due to not being trusted for binding"), while it loads Ghostty's, signed with a Team ID (`docs/human-checklist.md`, 6.2).

How it is checked:

- **Tests:** `pane_unittests`' `SettingsPersistenceTest` covers the setting's persistence (`RestoresAppIcon`) and Automatic's rule (`AutomaticAppIcon`), PaneKit's tests ported.
- **The `app-icon` scenario:** it runs each theme and setting against the window the Dock shows, through the automation snapshot, on Windows the window's. It opens the dialog, picks a sky with the dialog open, and closes it with Esc and with Return. It renders Settings' row and the dialog in each theme. In the test VM it reads Start's shortcut in the copy's home, as its first launch made it, after each of eight changes of Windows' theme each way, every second light change with the shortcut held mapped 1.5 s (`holdMapped`, as Windows holds it); on the engine before it fails at the first, which followed no shortcut in a test copy.
- **`windows-install`'s step 5** (the test VM): a light change with Start's own `Pane.lnk` held mapped 1.5 s, and the shortcut on Day once let go; on the engine before it stays on Night (commit `509811b`).
- **`pane_unittests`:** `WindowsIntegrationTest.AShortcutHeldMappedIsReportedAndRewrittenLater` (a mapped view: that shortcut counted as failed, the others rewritten, and rewritten once it is let go) and `ShortcutsFollowTheAppIcon`.
- **Its keys:** `app-icon` presses Esc and Return by real keys where the run lets Pane take the front, and `key-precedence` checks Quit Pane? over the dialog (ADR 0079).
- **Other scenarios:** `theme`, `smoke`, `shortcuts`, `quit`, `key-precedence`, and `security` pass on that build.
- **Windows' files against their boards:** each size read back by ImageIO, a reader apart from the writer, beside the board's own render of it (Paper 11.12.1 and 11.12.2's placement 1): the page, its fold, the window, and the tag where the board puts them at each size, Pane.ico's place, margin, and shadow those of the window the board draws; and in the test VM, chrome.exe's icon in Task Manager and Explorer, and the files at their sizes in Explorer, light and dark (2026-11-15, commit `220a9c7`).
- **The pixel comparisons:** `AppIconDay` against NSWorkspace's drawing of the packaged app shows no pixel apart, again with the whiter frame (2026-10-29), on a fresh copy of the app: macOS keeps an app's icon by its path, and the build's path still drew the old one. Day's icon against Sunset's differs by more than 8 levels only inside the glass, so the frames match: on the frame, every sky is within 3 levels of Day. Clouds' cartoon render keeps that (2026-10-29): its frame is Day's to the level, and it differs by more than 8 levels only inside the glass.
