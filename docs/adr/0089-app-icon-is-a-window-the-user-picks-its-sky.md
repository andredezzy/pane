# Pane's icon is a window whose sky the user picks, and the bundle carries it in Chromium's place, dated so macOS cannot show Chromium's

Pane's icon is a real window: a white enamel frame, two panes, and a mullion, with the sky behind the glass. It was rendered in Magnific with ten skies, and the user kept all ten and asked that Pane's users pick one (Paper: 01.2). The ten are Day, a clear blue sky with no sun, Clouds, Morning, Dusk, Fuchsia, Sunset, Twilight, Afterglow, Night, and Moonrise. Fuchsia carries the colours of a drawn direction the user liked (01.5, P2), which its render took as reference. The skies are cartoon ones, at the user's request (2026-10-29): round, puffy shapes with soft light and volume, each redone in Magnific from its earlier render, keeping its colours and its elements in the same places, and approved one by one (Paper: 01.15). Night is the exception, the realistic sky with fewer, cleaner stars, the quietest of five options after the user's "less is more" (Paper: 01.16); Sunset, given the same options, went back to its first cartoon. Twilight has three stars at different heights and sizes, its first, centred at the top of its pane, being too even (Paper: 01.17).

The user also asked that the icon follow light and dark. Settings › Appearance › App icon offers Automatic, the default, then the ten. Automatic shows Day while the app's appearance is light and Night while it is dark: the theme sets that appearance (ADR 0001), and under system the Mac does.

The row shows the chosen icon alone. A click opens a dialog over the window, as Quit Pane? is drawn (Paper: 06.2): a `ModalDialog` (`pane/ui/panels/app_icon_picker.cc`), made once after Settings' first frame. It holds every sky as a tile, six a row: the icon at 56 points over its name, the chosen one ringed as the profile sheet's swatch is. The ring sits 2 to 4 points out from the icon's body, on the system's corner (continuous, 0.257 of the side, measured on macOS 27). A click applies a sky at once and the Dock follows, so the dialog stays open to compare. Done, Return, Esc, or a click outside closes it (ADR 0079). Choosing is instant, and hover fades (DESIGN.md).

Every sky sits in the one frame: Day's, whitened. The user asked for whiter frames of the same measurements, and the renders' frames differed in tone, thickness, and opening, from a few pixels to a lilac or golden cast. So each render gives only its glass. Day's frame keeps its shading and its colour neutral, and is close to white: a shade's distance from white, d, becomes d²/200, so the broad faces whiten most and the bevels and the lip by the glass keep their depth. The curve answers the user's request for a whiter frame still (2026-10-29), where a lift of 45% toward white stopped at a mean luminance of 229 of 255, against 241. Each sky's panes are cut 20 pixels in from their outer sides, past the rounded corners' frame, and 3 from the mullion, so what sits by it stays (Sunset's sun, Moonrise's moon). They are stretched into Day's panes.

The bundle's icon is an Icon Composer document, `packaging/icon/AppIcon.icon`: Day, with Night as its dark appearance. Under the Mac's dark icon style (macOS 26 and later), Finder, Launchpad, and the Dock while Pane is closed show Night. Each render becomes a full-bleed layer whose frame edge sits 12 points past the canvas, so the system's squircle mask cuts inside the frame. macOS 26 and 27 shrink an icon that does not fill their squircle onto a grey plate. While Pane runs, `pane/ui/app/dock_icon.cc` gives the Dock the chosen sky, at its tile's 128 points, and under Automatic it follows Pane's light or dark: AppKit had drawn the whole 1024 pixels twice at each change, 15.6 ms of the UI thread, where this takes 1.45. Those images, `pane/ui/panels/app_icons/AppIcon<Sky>.png`, are macOS's own drawing of an app whose icon is that sky's window alone, so the running icon matches the bundle's to the pixel; they are carried in the framework's Resources (`base::DIR_ASSETS`) and decoded off the UI thread (`app_icon_images.cc`). `packaging/icon/make-icons.swift` makes all of them from `packaging/icon/renders`. It also makes the README's logo, Day on a light page and Night on a dark one.

The bundle carries this icon in Chromium's place. `scripts/package.sh` compiles `AppIcon.icon` with `actool` into a catalog of the icon alone (3 MB, its `Assets.car` and `AppIcon.icns`) and puts it in the app and in the two notification helpers, `Pane Helper (Alerts)` and `Pane Helper (Aperitif Alerts)`, whose icon notifications show: each loses Chromium's `app.icns`, and its `CFBundleIconFile` and `CFBundleIconName` name `AppIcon`. macOS caches an app's icon by its path and date, so `package.sh` gives every `.app` in the bundle the build's date and registers the app with LaunchServices (`lsregister -f`). The bundle names no document icon: the Swift build's asset catalog carried one, `Icon`, which no document type of the bundle named, 0.19.2's included, and it went with that catalog.

What was rejected:

- **An alternate icon API:** AppKit has none (iOS has `setAlternateIconName`).
- **A custom icon on the bundle** (`NSWorkspace.setIcon`): it writes into the signed app, and every update loses it.
- **The engine bundle's own dates:** `package.sh` copies it with `ditto`, which keeps them, so `Pane.app` kept the date it had when its icon was Chromium's (2026-10-26 04:12). Finder showed Pane's icon and LaunchServices had the right `AppIcon`, but the Dock kept Chromium's.
- **The app's catalog in the notification helpers:** with the Dock's skies, it would add 4.5 MB to each.
- **An asset catalog icon set with transparent corners:** macOS 26 and later jail it on the grey plate.
- **Runtime images exported with `ictool`:** they came out 2.8% brighter than the system's drawing of the same icon, a jump the Dock would show at launch.
- **A pop-up menu of the skies:** its thumbnails were too small to compare.
- **The tiles in the row itself:** the user found them heavy in Settings; they moved to the dialog.
- **Each render's own frame:** no two matched in tone or measure, and asking Magnific again matches none either.
- **More skies in Automatic:** the user asked for light and dark.
- **The skies the user turned down:** Day with the sun (Day lost it), a room reflected in a midday sky, a crescent moon with a blue glow on the frame, a moon behind clouds, a shooting star, and two oversized full moons.
- **A Dock tile plug-in for now** (`NSDockTilePlugIn`): it would keep the chosen sky in the Dock while Pane is closed. It waits for a Developer ID: on 2026-10-30 the Dock refused one in an ad-hoc signed copy ("Refusing to connect extra … due to not being trusted for binding"), while it loads Ghostty's, signed with a Team ID (`docs/human-checklist.md`, 6.2).

How it is checked:

- **Tests:** `pane_unittests`' `SettingsPersistenceTest` covers the setting's persistence (`RestoresAppIcon`) and Automatic's rule (`AutomaticAppIcon`), PaneKit's tests ported.
- **The `app-icon` scenario:** it runs each theme and setting against the window the Dock shows, through the automation snapshot. It opens the dialog, picks a sky with the dialog open, and closes it with Esc and with Return. It renders Settings' row and the dialog in each theme.
- **Its keys:** `app-icon` presses Esc and Return by real keys where the run lets Pane take the front, and `key-precedence` checks Quit Pane? over the dialog (ADR 0079).
- **Other scenarios:** `theme`, `smoke`, `shortcuts`, `quit`, `key-precedence`, and `security` pass on that build.
- **The pixel comparisons:** `AppIconDay` against NSWorkspace's drawing of the packaged app shows no pixel apart, again with the whiter frame (2026-10-29), on a fresh copy of the app: macOS keeps an app's icon by its path, and the build's path still drew the old one. Day's icon against Sunset's differs by more than 8 levels only inside the glass, so the frames match: on the frame, every sky is within 3 levels of Day. Clouds' cartoon render keeps that (2026-10-29): its frame is Day's to the level, and it differs by more than 8 levels only inside the glass.
