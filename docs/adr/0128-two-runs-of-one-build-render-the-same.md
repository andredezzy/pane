# Two runs of one build render the same, byte for byte: a test copy's pointer, clock, carets, ports, loads, downloads, and loops are pinned

Two runs of one build, behind the user's windows, give renders identical in every RGBA byte, whatever the clock, the fixtures' ports, or the user's own pointer do, so a strict comparison of two builds' renders (`tests/rigs/compare-renders.ts`) tells a change from noise, and "every surface matches its Paper board and the last release's render, in every state" rests on renders that mean the same each time. Each cause a run left to chance is pinned where it starts, for a scenario's copy alone (the mock keychain and the channel, ADR 0115): nothing changes for a person's Pane. Decided 2026-11-18 (W10d), after W8a's and W8e's comparisons found a third of the renders differing between runs (`evidence/views-migration/runs/p5-w8a-notes.md`, `p5-w8e-notes.md`).

## What is pinned, and where

- **The pointer is the channel's.** Views asks the screen where the pointer is when a button shows (`Button::VisibilityChanged` → `View::IsMouseHovered`), when a scroll moves what's under it (`Widget::SynthesizeMouseMoveEvent`), and when a menu opens (`MenuController`). On the Mac that is the real pointer's place (`[NSEvent mouseLocation]`), whatever window is in front: a key of the PIN pad, drawn by a copy behind the user's windows under the user's pointer, showed hovered in one run and not the next, and the user's pointer can't be asked to stay away. While the channel runs (`pane/automation/channel_pointer.h`), a stand-in for the system's `display::Screen`, put in its place as Chromium's own tests put theirs (`Screen::SetScreenInstance`), answers with where the channel's last click, move, scroll, or drag left the pointer, and before the first with a point on no screen; it asks the system's screen for everything else (displays, their observers, windows at a point). The input functions (`input.h`, each system's) and the drag's phases move it, and on the Mac a click moves there first, as Windows' click does (`native_input_mac.mm`): without the move, Views never knew a click's control was under the pointer, and never sent it its exit, so a control a click left hovered stayed so once a hover came back to it. And while the copy may not take the front (`MayTakeFront`), the system's own pointer events are kept from it (`channel_pointer_mac.mm`, a local monitor dropping moves, enters, exits, drags, and scrolls as AppKit dequeues them; the channel's moves and scrolls go to the window itself, past it): a copy is active for a moment as it launches, and a window behind takes the pointer where it shows, so the user's pointer resting over the sidebar's foot lit New in one run, and over the pad a key. In front (`--foreground`, a pass by hand) the pointer is a person's and nothing is dropped; Windows' side drops nothing yet, its copies running in the test VM, where no one moves the pointer. So a copy's hover follows the channel's pointer alone, as a person's follows their hand, and a render of a state at rest moves it off the controls first (`surfaces`' every render, `update-states`' all but its hovers, `views-toolbar`'s `pill-downloading`, `views-sidebar`'s Settings, `security`'s last attempt, as `profile-sheet` and the rigs' `away` already did). In `//pane` with no patch (ADR 0004): `Screen` is the seam Chromium keeps for this.
- **The time of day shown is the runner's.** The update check's "checked at" read the run's clock in every Settings and App icon render. `PANE_SHOWN_TIME` (`pane/model/test_hooks.h`, seconds since 1970) is the moment every time of day Pane shows stands for; the runner sets 09:41 of this computer's clock (`tests/lib/pane.ts`). Only what is shown takes it: what Pane keeps and counts with, the check's schedule included, stays the clock's.
- **Carets don't blink.** A field with the keys showed its caret in one run and not in the next. While the channel runs, the native theme's blink interval is none (`pane/automation/steady_caret.h`), as macOS draws a caret for a user who prefers one that doesn't blink, and Windows at a blink rate of None; Views' fields and Chrome's pages both read it (`Textfield::ShouldBlinkCursor`, `renderer_preferences_util.cc`), and it is set again after a change of the system's settings.
- **The runner's servers keep their ports.** The fixtures' and a scenario's proxies' ports show in a sign-in sheet, the profile sheet's proxy, and the address field. Each takes the first free port from its family's, the fixtures from 41000 and a scenario's servers from 41100 in the order it starts them, below the ports the system hands out to connections (`tests/lib/ports.ts`); one taken, by another app, moves it on and shows in that run's renders.
- **What loads is loaded.** A render waits for what it shows: `sidebar-resize`'s 24 tabs loaded and titled before the list is captured, where rows said Loading… in one run and their titles in the next.
- **A download shown under way is held.** The stand-in releases stop the next download at a set share until the rig has rendered it (`Fixtures.holdNextDownload`, `serveSlowRelease`'s `hold`), and the render waits past the bar's move there (Motion.progress, 0.2 s): the bar's end, its percent, and its time left are the same in every run.
- **The download's dot holds at full.** It breathes every two seconds while a download runs, and a render caught it anywhere in its breath. While the channel runs it holds at full (`UpdateDot::HoldStill`), as under the system's Reduce motion, which a user can choose.
- **What moves has stopped.** A render after a drag waits past the slide's spring (`views-reorder`, 1 s: it settles 0.37 s on from rest and later from a move under way), and the rows' places a drag starts from are read at rest; the Web Store's page is captured once its fonts are in and two frames drawn, where its text wasn't drawn yet in one run.
- **An overlay scroll bar is gone before a render.** Views shows it at every scroll, programmatic too, and fades it half a second on (`CocoaScrollBar`, 240 ms; Windows' a second on, 200 ms): a render after a scroll waits `SCROLL_BAR_GONE_MS`.

What stays, a moment of motion by design: the slow load's shimmer (`toolbar-loading-slow`, in `surfaces` and `views-toolbar`), a slide caught 70 ms in (`reorder-tab-sliding`), and the data move under way (`lock-data-move`, the bar's end). Each shows a frame of something moving, which the clock of the frame decides; pinning one would take a clock of the scenario's for that motion, stepped by the channel (the progress bar's frames, Pane's springs, the vault's move), engine code for the three renders. Two builds' renders of them are compared by eye. And one way the user's pointer still reaches a copy: while a Views menu is open, Chromium's mouse capture on the Mac adds a global event monitor (`CocoaMouseCapture`, `mouse_capture.mm`), which hands the menu the pointer's moves anywhere on the screen, and Views passes one over the window under the menu on to it; a local monitor can't drop a global one's events. Keeping it out would take a hunk in `mouse_capture.mm`, not taken (ADR 0004): it showed once in two runs, in three renders of `extensions-menu`.

## Why

- **A regression and noise looked the same.** W8a's strict comparison of two builds found 71 of 235 renders differing, W8e's 74 of 241, each examined by hand and put down to the run: no later round could prove an unchanged surface unchanged without doing the same. With every cause pinned a difference is the build's.
- **At the source, not over the pixels.** Masking a region, or a tolerance, hides a regression in the same pixels: the clock's digits sit in the same row as the update's state, and a hover in the same key as its pressed look.

## Rejected

- **A tolerance or masks** (`tests/tools/image-diff.swift`'s 2/255, a box over the clock or the port): they hide what they cover.
- **Mouse events off for a test copy's widgets** (`Widget::IsMouseEventsEnabled`): it ends the channel's own hovers, which the rigs render (`pin-key-hover`, `lock-update-hover`).
- **A patch to Chromium's screen** (`ScreenMac::GetCursorScreenPoint`): `//pane` can do it through the seam Chromium keeps (ADR 0004).
- **Moving the test window where the user's pointer can't go,** off the screen: macOS keeps a window on a screen, and the user's pointer goes everywhere.
- **The update check's own clock pinned:** it would change when a copy checks, which `updates` and `update-timing` check.
- **Every animation stopped in test copies** (`ScopedAnimationDurationScaleMode`): the scenarios time and check motion, and the scroll bar's half second is a timer, not an animation.
- **Always-shown scroll bars in test copies:** a look the user's Pane doesn't have, against the boards.
- **Random ports mapped to one name** (`--host-resolver-rules`): every fixture URL changes, for the same result.

## How it's checked

`bun tests/rigs/compare-renders.ts` over two runs of the render set on one package, behind, light and dark, the user at the Mac and moving the pointer (`evidence/views-migration/runs/p6-w10d-notes.md`): on `main` 160 of 248 light renders and 177 of 248 dark were identical; with the pins 241 of 246 light and 239 of 248 dark, what differs being the motion above, the menu's capture, and two renders of a profile dragged (`reorder-profile-dragging`, `-open-dragging`), whose copy and rows stand about a pixel apart from one run to the next even with every slide waited out: their cause is not found yet. `pane_unittests`' `TestHooksTest.ShownTime` and `NothingOffTheMockKeychain` for the hook; `tests/lib/png.test.ts` for the comparer's reader.
