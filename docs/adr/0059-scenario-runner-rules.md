# The scenario runner cleans up, keeps deadlines, and waits for conditions

The scenario runner (`tests/run.ts`) cleans up after each scenario, gives each one a deadline, and has each check wait for the condition it needs rather than a fixed time, so a run leaves the machine as it found it and a failure is the app's, not the runner's. These rules came from the runner's review (S1–S7 and S9). Two more came from scenarios that failed on their own: a scenario reads a control again before it acts on it, and clicks where the controls are now.

A scenario owns its temp dirs (`context.tempDir(prefix)`), `launchPane`'s data dir among them. When it ends, pass or fail, the runner kills whatever still uses them, found by the dir in a process's arguments (`tests/lib/processes.ts`), and removes them. The data dir had leaked on every launch: 515 `pane-data-*` dirs and 11 GB in `$TMPDIR`. In a private `$TMPDIR`, six scenarios (`smoke quit security restore profile-lifecycle downloads`) left 7 dirs and 112 MB under the old runner, and none under the new one. A failed launch kills its app and removes its dirs, and an app that exits before serving DevTools fails it at once: `/usr/bin/false` took 60,134 ms to fail, and now takes 261 ms. The CDP client rejects pending calls and waiters once its socket closes, and `Browser.close` is skipped for an app that already quit: `quit`'s teardown had waited out a 15 s timeout and takes 85 ms now, so `smoke quit` went from 23.7 s to 6 s. The teardown also stops the panic wipe's new instance (`security`), which is no child of the runner and had been stopped only if every check before it passed.

Each scenario has a deadline: `--timeout`, 600 s by default. A scenario past it fails, its apps and dirs go, and the run goes on. `report.json` is written however a scenario ends (ADR 0012 has what it records). Under `--timeout 3`, a scenario that never returns failed at 3,002 ms, and the next one ran. Rejections a scenario leaves behind are logged, not fatal.

Each helper has one copy. `tests/lib/targets.ts` finds DevTools targets, in place of five hand-written loops; `waitForExit`, and `launchTraced` with `waitForEntry`, replace hand-parsed traces; there is one `windowsOf`. `tests/lib/tools.ts` builds every Swift tool, and builds it again once its source is newer: an edited tool used to keep running its old build from `$TMPDIR`.

A check waits for what it needs, with `tests/lib/poll.ts` and `PaneApp.settled`. `ubol` loads its page again until the rules block the ads, where it waited 3 s twice. `sidebar-reorder` reads the order once the drop has changed it, not 400 ms after. `hung-renderer` types once the page names itself Hanging, not 2 s after the load. `password-managers` presses its button once no more tabs open (2 s quiet), not after 5 s, and branches on what the press opened, since 1Password turns its popup off seconds after installing. Full screen's Esc, the lock panel's hit test, the DevTools frontend, ⌘R's count, and `http-auth`'s fields wait the same way. The fixed waits that stay prove that something does not happen (`quiet`, `migration`, `external-protocol`, `dialog-beforeunload`, `password-manager-off`, `extensions-per-profile`, `window-chrome`, `sleep`), or let an animation finish before an evidence capture. Three wait for something no signal shows: the spellchecker's verdict (`context-menu`), and the key-status flicker a new tab or a closed popup causes (`extensions-menu`). 68 sleeps became 50, loops included.

A scenario reads a control again before it acts on it. `extensions-menu` reopens the more menu before pressing a row: it had failed twice when the window gave up key status mid-run (an extension's tab or popup taking it while the app is not frontmost), which closes the menu, as it should. It passed three runs in a row after. `clickControl` reads an accessibility element again and presses, up to three times, when AppKit refuses one SwiftUI has replaced since the read (kAXErrorInvalidUIElement, -25202), and for that error alone; `extensions-page-profile` had failed once in five runs on it.

A scenario clicks where the controls are now. When the sidebar widened to Aside's 260 points (ADR 0060), `window-chrome`'s click at the old back position landed on Collapse all. It clicks back at (281, 29) and Collapse all at (236, 19.5).
