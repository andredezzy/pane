# The view layer has one source per concern

Each design concern in Pane's views has one source that every view uses (the colors, the shapes, the shadows, the icons, the motion, and one style per kind of control), so controls of one kind look and behave alike and a change lands once. The sources are `pane/ui/design/`, and DESIGN.md says how each is used:

- **Colors:** `palette.h`, every color a view draws, with the status colors, the modal scrim, and the separators (`Hairline`), each in its own color; the theme is `theme.cc` (ADR 0001). A field's selection is the system's (`kPaneColorTextSelection`, AppKit's colors in the appearance shown, following the user's Highlight color), dimmed to its unemphasized one while the window isn't key (`SelectionFollowsWindow`), where Chromium's own ended on Material's tonal container in dark.
- **Shapes:** `squircle.h`, every corner on the radius scale (ADR 0057), and `focus_ring.h`.
- **Cards and shadows:** `dialog.h`. A dialog's card is one `DialogCard`, its fill, edge, shadow, and entrance in one layer, which `ModalDialog` (Quit Pane?, the App icon picker, with `DialogHeading`) and a page's dialog take with their own looks, and the extensions menus with the popover's, the switcher's surface (ADR 0055). A dialog's scrim is a color the compositor draws, and its shadow a layer sized to its reach.
- **Icons:** `icons.h`, Lucide's only (`third_party/lucide`), and `menu_icon.h` for a menu's.
- **Motion:** `motion.h`, every curve named for what it is (`Hover`, `HoverTurn`, `Morph`, `Slide`, `Reveal`, `Pulse`, `Dot`, `ShakeOffset`, and the rest), moved on the compositor's frames (`LayerMotion`, `CompositorMotion`, `ValueMotion`). While the system reduces motion every change is at once, so a view honors the setting with no check of its own (`IsReduced`). A spring that takes over a change under way starts as fast as it was moving, as SwiftUI merges one spring into another, so a dragged row heading for a new place never stops dead (`Curve::At`); each spring is worked out once. A change's clock starts a frame back (`FirstFrameStart`), so its first frame shows a frame's progress, as Core Animation's did: Quit Pane? came 21.2 ms to the frame on screen before, and 14.2 after.
- **Controls:** each kind has one, which owns its hover, press, and disabled look (`controls.h`): `TextButton` for every text button, with Aside's popup buttons (`kPopupPrimary`, `kPopupCancel`, at `button_size::kPopup`), an alert's (`kAlert`, `kAlertDefault`: capsules, a 12% press, no hover), and the keyed ones of Cancel and the destructive Quit (`KeyedButton`); `IconButton`, with a look and a held state (`SetLook`, `SetHeld`: the puzzle lit while its menu shows, the pin's blue); `MenuButton`, the one menu of options, which opens the system's menu under itself with the current option checked (`AnchoredMenu`); `Field`; `Checkbox`, the system's own (AppKit's cell drawn offscreen on the Mac, the native theme's on Windows); `Switch`; the fading scroll view (`scroll_fade.h`, ADR 0061); and the update's dot and bar (`update_status.h`), which the sidebar's pill, the Lock's update line, and the data's move on the Lock's panel take. A press lays the foreground at 6% over the fill (the card at 10% over a primary or destructive one) in the next frame and fades out as it lets go, so the click's own effect, a selection, lands at once; a disabled icon button is at 40%.

It came from a review of the Swift app's view layer, which split its `Theme.swift` and `Style.swift` by concern, took every color into one palette and every animation into one `Motion` table that honored reduced motion in one place (eighteen copies of the 0.15-second literal went, and the animations that had ignored the setting honored it), and gave each kind of button one style, rendering every surface before and after, light and dark. The move to Views kept each concern's one source, in C++ (ADR 0124), and DESIGN.md's rules with it.
