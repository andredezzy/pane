# The sidebar is resizable, and its list fades at the edges

The sidebar's width follows a drag of its edge, at the user's request, and its list fades at its top and bottom edges as it scrolls, as the Electron app's `scroll-fade-y` did, after the user's report that rows cut hard under the header. Both were designed in Paper first (03.11 Sidebar · Scroll & resize; ADR 0058).

**The edge** is a `views::ResizeArea` 8 points wide, 5 over the sidebar and 3 over the panel, under every overlay (`pane/ui/sidebar/resize_handle.h`). A drag follows the pointer between 200 and 400 points with no animation, as pointer-driven changes are (DESIGN.md › Motion). A double-click returns to 260, Aside's width (ADR 0060). The cursor is the column cursor, pointing the one way left at either limit on macOS 15 and later (`resize_cursor_mac.mm`), where AppKit has one-way column cursors; before 15, the two-way one throughout. To accessibility, the edge is a splitter valued at the width, which steps by 10 (ADR 0071). The width is kept across launches in `settings.json` (`model::AppSettings`' `sidebar_width`, clamped as it loads), and a drag writes the file once, as it ends, not at every step.

**The fades.** The top fade grows from 0 to 40 points over the first 40 points scrolled, and the bottom one shrinks to 0 over the last 40. At first they were 24 points and linear, the Electron app's; the user asked for them larger and stronger (2026-11-04), so they are 40, and the content's alpha is the square of its share of the way in from the edge: half way in it shows at 25%, where it showed at 50%. Nothing fades at either end, or when the list fits, and a window shrunk under a list that fit fades too. They are two overlays in the color of what is behind the list (`FadingScrollView`, `pane/ui/design/scroll_fade.h`), not a mask, which would make a render surface of the list, so the app's own render shows them; the Swift app masked the list. A fade is for an edge with nothing to mark it (the user's rule, DESIGN.md › Scroll edges): the profile sheet's body, between its two rules, takes none (ADR 0120). Paper's sidebars all take the bottom fade, as they draw the list scrolled to its top.

`sidebar-resize` drags through the window's own events (`dragWindow`, which hands the press, the moves, and the release to the root view as Views' events, since a widget routes moves to the pressed view only while a hardware button is down), checks the width at each end and after a relaunch, and times each step from its event to the window's layout at the new width: over 25 rows, 0.11 ms at the median (p95 0.17, max 0.6), where the Swift app's took 3.4 to 4.8 ms (p95 4.4 to 6.2), its cost laying out the rows, not the fade. It scrolls the list (`scrollWindow`) and captures the window at the top and scrolled, where the screen can be read (ADR 0012). For these checks the automation channel (ADR 0018) gained `dragWindow`, `scrollWindow` (a trackpad scroll at a point), and `clicks` for `clickWindow` (a double-click).
