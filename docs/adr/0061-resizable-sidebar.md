# The sidebar is resizable, and its list fades at the edges

The sidebar's width follows a drag of its edge, at the user's request, and its list fades at its top and bottom edges as it scrolls, as the Electron app's `scroll-fade-y` did, after the user's report that rows cut hard under the header. Both were designed in Paper first (03.11 Sidebar · Scroll & resize; ADR 0058).

The edge is 8 points wide, 5 over the sidebar and 3 over the panel, under every overlay (`SidebarResizeHandle`). A drag follows the pointer between 200 and 400 points with no animation, as pointer-driven changes are (DESIGN.md › Motion). A double-click returns to 260, Aside's width (ADR 0060). The cursor is macOS 15's column cursor, pointing the one way left at either limit (`resizeLeftRight` before 15). To accessibility, the edge is a splitter valued at the width, which steps by 10. The width is kept across launches in settings.json (`AppSettings.sidebarWidth`, clamped as it loads), and a drag writes the file once, as it ends, not at every step.

The top fade grows from 0 to 24 points over the first 24 points scrolled, and the bottom one shrinks to 0 over the last 24. Nothing fades at either end, or when the list fits. `FadingScrollView` reads the content's frame in the scroll view and masks it. Paper's sidebars all take the bottom fade, as they draw the list scrolled to its top.

`sidebar-resize` drags through the window's own events and times each step from its event to the window's layout at the new width. With one tab, the median step is 1.05 ms (p95 2.1, max 4.6). With 25 tabs in the list it is 3.4 to 4.8 ms (p95 4.4 to 6.2, max 8.7 to 9.6), on a machine at a load of 5.7. Without the fade it is 4.2 to 5.0, so the cost is laying out the rows, not the fade. The app's own render leaves masks out (`CALayer.render(in:)` draws no layer masks), so the scenario captures the window from the screen to show the fade, when the screen is unlocked (ADR 0012). For these checks the automation channel (ADR 0018) gained `dragWindow` (a drag in steps, each timed to layout), `scrollWindow` (a trackpad scroll at a point), and `clicks` for `clickWindow` (a double-click).
