# The address bar's progress is Chrome's toolbar progress bar, for the selected tab

The address bar's load progress is a port of the bar stable Chrome draws, and it shows the selected tab's own load alone: the user's call was to reverse-engineer a stable browser's bar and work exactly as it does. The reference is Chromium's own, read in the local checkout: Chrome for Android's `ToolbarProgressBar`, with its `LoadProgressMediator`, `ProgressAnimationSmooth`, and `ToolbarProgressBarAnimatingView`, as stable Chrome runs it (its composited animation, `AndroidAnimatedProgressBarInBrowser`, is off by default). `ToolbarProgress` (`app/PaneKit/Sources/PaneKit/ToolbarProgress.swift`) is the port, with Chrome's constants, and `ToolbarProgressBar` (`app/Pane/Views/Components/ToolbarProgressBar.swift`) draws it, on the display's frames while something moves.

A load shows its real progress at once, at least 5%, fading in over 140 ms (Material's linear-out-slow-in), and jumps to each new value. Five seconds with no news start the slow mode: a shimmer crosses the filled part, slow then fast, ln(width) × 260 ms a pass and a second apart, growing from 10% to 30% of the filled width, and the fill moves on a constant acceleration and deceleration of 0.15 a second squared. A finished load fills the bar (in the slow mode racing there at 7 a second squared), holds 100 ms, and fades out over 140 ms (fast-out-linear-in). A tab come forward with no load, a page's crash, or its page released hides the bar at once; a tab come forward mid-load shows its own progress. A load is the main frame loading a new document (ADR 0081).

The bar has followed the selected tab since a study of how Aside, Dia, Safari, and Chrome show a load (the web, and Aside's and Dia's binaries, read-only): a tab coming forward shows its own state at once, never an animation of the last one's, where the bar had played the last tab's finish (a fill and a fade) over a loaded tab. Dia keys on the frame tree's loading-UI state and draws its bar in Metal. Aside's bar lives in its `LocationBarView`: fake progress, 2% each 500 ms up to 90%, held a second at 100% before it fades, and a switch to a loaded tab hides it at once. Chrome for Android fills to 100%, holds 100 ms, and fades in 140 ms. Safari's starts at 10% and stays under 90% until the load is done.

The port replaced the Electron app's bar, 8 seconds of made-up progress to 90%, along with the 5% start and the resumed curve for a tab come forward that followed the study (`loadingSince`, gone). One difference is on purpose: Chrome's mediator keeps its last progress across loads, so a load starting at the value the last one ended at drew an empty bar until its next value; this one draws it. The look stays Pane's: the blue-500 to blue-400 gradient, 2 points under the address, and no track (Chrome's has one). While the Mac reduces motion, the fades are instant.

Ten PaneKit tests check it, one per rule. `progress-bar` holds a load 9 s at the server: the bar starts at 10%, a tab with no load hides it at once, the loading tab shows its 10% again, the shimmer starts 5.2 s after the last news, and the end fills the bar and fades it. In captured frames, the finish in the slow mode filled in 0.46 s (7 a second squared predicts 0.51), held, and was gone 0.25 s later.
