# AppKit places the traffic lights at (12, 12), from the window's first frame

The window's chrome is laid out as the Electron app's, and AppKit itself puts the traffic lights where the Electron app had them, at (12, 12) in the sidebar's 39-point header (Electron's `trafficLightPosition`), from the window's first frame and through full screen, so no layout of AppKit's moves them. The content runs under the hidden title bar, which spans the top 38 points, over the toolbar; real clicks there still reach the toolbar and the header. The window is 1280 × 800 by default, 900 × 600 at least, and AppKit's title bar is left alone in full screen.

The lights are placed as Chrome's own browser window places them. Its window class answers `frameViewClassForStyleMask:` with a frame view, `BrowserWindowFrame`, that answers NSThemeFrame's `_titlebarHeight`, `_minXTitlebarWidgetInset`, and `_shouldCenterTrafficLights`, so AppKit's every layout puts the buttons where it wants. The engine's window is Chromium's plain `NativeWidgetMacNSWindow`, so before the engine makes the app's window, `TitleBar.prepare()` (`app/Pane/Window/TitleBar.swift`) has that class answer with a subclass of its titled frame view. Every frame view the window gets is then one, including those AppKit builds anew when the style mask changes, as full screen's does. The subclass answers 38 points, 12 in, and centered for the app's window alone, and AppKit's own values for any other window and in full screen. Once the window exists, `TitleBar.install` lays its title bar out at once (`_tileTitlebarAndRedisplay:`, what a new title does), before the window is first drawn.

Simpler placements failed, each on one of the user's reports. AppKit lays the title bar out again for every new title, and the window's title follows the page's, so the buttons went back to AppKit's own (9, 9): they flicked. Moving them back whenever their frames changed fought that layout, and left the zoom button at AppKit's (55, 9), off the others' line. A frame view swapped into the subclass, as KVO swaps a class, was lost whenever a style mask change made AppKit build the frame view anew from the window's class, so out of full screen the lights went back to (9, 9) for good; and at launch they sat there for 1.7 seconds, until the first page's title laid the title bar out, and behind the PIN lock until it opened. Swapping the window's own class instead was rejected: it broke AppKit's observers of the window, with an exception on the next style mask change, so the window keeps Chromium's.

The sidebar's title sits level with the lights (the user's report: it sat low). The header centered it on its own middle, 19.5 points down, while the lights' center is about 18.75 (measured at 2×: the lights at 37.5 px, the title's ink at 39 px). A point of padding under the title moves it up half a point, to 38 px, a quarter point from the lights' center: as close as whole pixels allow, since the lights span an even number of pixels and the title's ink an odd one.

The window moves by its background, where the Electron app's drag regions let it (`app/Pane/Views/Components/WindowDragArea.swift`): its root and the lock screen's panel were `-webkit-app-region: drag`, and its controls, the sidebar's list, and the content panel were not. The window is Chrome's, whose title bar moves it only where Chrome's own views report a caption, and the app's SwiftUI covers all of it, so nothing moved it. A view under the content now starts the drag itself, with `performDrag(with:)`, as Chrome's frame view does with `performWindowDragWithEvent:`. A double-click zooms or minimizes per the system setting (`AppleActionOnDoubleClick`).

`window-chrome` reads all three buttons' frames right after launch, before any title (it read (9, 9) before the last fix), after a navigation changes the title, and with the pointer over the zoom button, and its real clicks under the title bar reach the toolbar and the header. `fullscreen` checks that the lights end at (12, 12) after each way in and out of full screen (ADR 0025). Watching the buttons' frames every 4 ms through launch, tabs, the profile sheet, Settings, themes, the lock screen, find, hover, resizes, DevTools, and zoom had found no move but the one at launch. `window-drag` hit-tests the window: the header, its title included, the gutters around the content panel, and the lock panel move it; the traffic lights, the page, the sidebar's list, Settings, and the numpad keep their clicks.
