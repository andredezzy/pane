# AppKit places the traffic lights at (12, 12), from the window's first frame

The window's chrome is laid out as the Electron app's, and AppKit itself puts the traffic lights where the Electron app had them, at (12, 12) in the sidebar's 39-point header (Electron's `trafficLightPosition`), from the window's first frame and through full screen, so no layout of AppKit's moves them. The content runs under the hidden title bar, which spans the top 38 points, over the toolbar; real clicks there still reach the toolbar and the header. The window is 1280 × 800 by default, 900 × 600 at least, and AppKit's title bar is left alone in full screen.

**The lights are placed as Chrome's own browser window places them.** Pane's window is of its own class (`ViewsWindowForPane`, `pane/ui/window/frame_mac.mm`), as Chrome's browser window is of its own (`browser_native_widget_window_mac.mm`): it answers `frameViewClassForStyleMask:` with a frame view (`ViewsTitledFrameForPane`) that answers NSThemeFrame's `_titlebarHeight`, `_minXTitlebarWidgetInset`, and `_shouldCenterTrafficLights`, so AppKit's every layout puts the buttons where it wants: 38 points tall (twice 12 plus a light's height), 12 in, centered, and AppKit's own values in full screen. Every frame view the window gets is one, including those AppKit builds anew when the style mask changes, as full screen's does. The frame sets what Chromium's own window creation would (a transparent title bar, its title hidden, not released when closed, no restoration, ADR 0016).

Simpler placements failed, each on one of the user's reports. AppKit lays the title bar out again for every new title, and the window's title follows the page's, so the buttons went back to AppKit's own (9, 9): they flicked. Moving them back whenever their frames changed fought that layout, and left the zoom button at AppKit's (55, 9), off the others' line. A frame view swapped into a subclass, as KVO swaps a class, was lost whenever a style mask change made AppKit build the frame view anew from the window's class, so out of full screen the lights went back to (9, 9) for good; and at launch they sat there for 1.7 seconds, until the first page's title laid the title bar out, and behind the PIN lock until it opened. Swapping a live window's own class was rejected: it broke AppKit's observers of the window, with an exception on the next style mask change. The Swift app, whose window the engine made as Chromium's plain `NativeWidgetMacNSWindow`, had that class answer with a subclass of its titled frame view before the window was made (`TitleBar.swift`); the Views app makes its window of its own class from the start.

The sidebar's title sits level with the lights (the user's report: it sat low). Centered on the header's own middle, 19.5 points down, it sat below the lights' center, about 18.75 (measured at 2×: the lights at 37.5 px, the title's ink at 39 px). Centered a point above the header's bottom, it moves up half a point, to 38 px, a quarter point from the lights' center (`pane/ui/sidebar/sidebar_view.cc`): as close as whole pixels allow, since the lights span an even number of pixels and the title's ink an odd one.

**The window moves by its background,** where the Electron app's drag regions let it: its root and the lock screen's panel were `-webkit-app-region: drag`, and its controls, the sidebar's list, and the content panel were not. The root view answers `HTCAPTION` there (`RootView::IsDragArea`, through `Window::NonClientHitTest`): the sidebar's header, its title included, the gutters around the content panel, and, while it shows, the PIN screen's panel around its controls; Chromium's frame view turns a press there into the window's drag. A double-click there zooms or minimizes per the system setting (`AppleActionOnDoubleClick`), in Chromium's own handling of the window's events, read in its code and not yet pressed in a run. In the Swift app, the window was Chrome's, whose title bar moves it only where Chrome's own views report a caption, and SwiftUI covered all of it, so nothing moved it until a view under the content started the drag itself (`WindowDragArea`, with `performDrag(with:)`).

`window-chrome` reads all three buttons' frames right after launch, before any title (it read (9, 9) before the fix), after a navigation changes the title, and with the pointer over the zoom button, and its real clicks under the title bar reach the toolbar and the header. `fullscreen` checks that the lights end at (12, 12) after each way in and out of full screen (ADR 0025). Watching the buttons' frames every 4 ms through launch, tabs, the profile sheet, Settings, themes, the lock screen, find, hover, resizes, DevTools, and zoom had found no move in the Swift app but the one at launch. `window-drag` hit-tests the window: the header, its title included, the gutters around the content panel, and the lock panel move it; the traffic lights, the page, the sidebar's list, Settings, and the numpad keep their clicks.
