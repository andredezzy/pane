# Pane's views redraw only what changed, never mask the page, and paint on layers no bigger than what they draw

Pane's views redraw only what changed, and nothing Pane draws masks the page or animates it in, so the page keeps its own compositing and a covered page costs nothing. Four rules make it so.

**The sidebar's rows take values.** The sidebar (`pane/ui/sidebar/`) shows what the app gives it as values (`SidebarContent`), keeps its views by profile and tab, and a row whose value didn't change does nothing at a refresh. A drag repaints no row: the rows it reorders slide on layers of their own (`RowList`), its copy rides the pointer on another (`DragCopy`), and a new order only restacks those layers; once the drop's fade ends, the rows paint into the list again (ADR 0066). The active profile's bar still morphs (ADR 0064).

**Nothing masks the page.** The root view paints the content panel's card, ring, and shadow under the page's `WebView`, and the page's own layer takes the panel's corners as its radii (`RootView::RoundPage`, `SetNativeViewCornerRadii`, ADR 0057), square while the page fills the window, so nothing clips it or composites a mask over it each frame. Settings draws in the panel with no fill of its own over the card, on a layer of its own whose corners are fast rounded corners. The empty state shows only with no page in the panel. The App icon picker's scrim and card are layers of the window's one compositor.

**A page under Settings or a page of Chrome's is hidden.** Settings takes the panel, and the selected tab's page leaves it, hidden (`RootView::ShowPlaces`), so its renderer is told, as a background tab is, and draws nothing under Settings: hidden in place, it had stayed visible to the engine as the window's active tab. Chrome's extensions page opens in a tab of its own (ADR 0062), so the page behind it is a background tab. A tab switch shows the next page at once, with no fade (DESIGN.md › Motion).

**Each view paints on a layer no bigger than what it draws.** The window's own layer holds only its color, so it keeps no tiles. The content panel's card, ring, and shadow are four bands along the card's edge, each a layer that paints the whole frame clipped to itself, so its pixels are a single painting's, and a middle the compositor fills (`PanelFrame`, `pane/ui/window/panel_frame.h`); the toolbar, the sidebar's header, footer, and rows, the empty state, and the sad tab draw on layers no bigger than what they draw. cc tiles a painted layer wherever a path is drawn in it, in stripes as wide as the window when it rasters on the GPU, so the card painted on the window's layer kept 16 MB of tiles in the GPU process at rest, and the list's contents, which the scroll view keeps as tall as the list, 3 MB more (`docs/benchmarks/views-shell.md`). Pane's views now raster on the CPU (ADR 0123).

**How it came about,** in the Swift app, where each rule was first measured. Its sidebar's sections and rows compared their values (`.equatable()`) so SwiftUI ran only the changed ones: a profile section had taken an edit closure SwiftUI cannot compare, and every tab row read the model's selection itself; counted with each body logging itself, selecting a tab in another profile, of 24 tabs in three, ran 24 tab rows and 3 sections, then 2 and 2, and a tab navigating ran 6 sections, then 2. SwiftUI had clipped the panel, page included, to its squircle by hosting the page in an `_NSGraphicsView` with a `MaskLayer`, the per-frame mask the page's own rounded layer was there to avoid; read from the layer tree with a probe build, the mask went and the hosting view's masked sublayers went from 16 to 4, and the panel's edge pixels, captured from the screen, differed by at most 14 of 255. Over an animating page under Settings, the page drew 480 frames in 4 seconds, and 0 once it was hidden; the GPU helper's CPU went from 0.24 seconds in 5 to 0.03, and the renderer's from 0.31 to 0. A tab switch faded its page in over about a quarter second (its background at 172, 241, then 244 of 255 across screen captures), a selection that animated, from Core Animation's implicit actions on the views going in and out, until they were turned off.

How it is checked: `navigation` reads the selected page's `document.visibilityState` under Settings and under Chrome's extensions page, hidden, and back, visible; it failed before. The tiles and the GPU process's memory were measured against 0.19.2 (`docs/benchmarks/views-shell.md`).
