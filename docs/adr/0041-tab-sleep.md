# Idle tabs sleep and hidden profiles unload on PaneKit's scheduler, with Chrome's discarding off

Idle tabs sleep and hidden profiles unload on PaneKit's port of the Electron app's `sleep-scheduler.ts` (`app/PaneKit/Sources/PaneKit/SleepScheduler.swift`), so Sleep, Unload, Trim, and Keep loaded work as CONTEXT.md defines them and as the Electron app's did. Chrome's own tab discarding is off: Pane's sleep does that job, and Chrome's swapped a tab's WebContents out from under the app. The port keeps every case of the Electron scheduler's tests and has an injectable clock. The app's timer ticks it every minute (`app/Pane/Browser/SleepController.swift`), and `PANE_TIME_SCALE` speeds the clock up for the `sleep` scenario.

Sleep releases a tab's page, as in the Electron app: the page closes and the tab stays, dimmed. Selecting the tab navigates it again, so its back and forward history does not survive. Chrome's in-place discard (`WebContents::Discard`) would keep the history, but on desktop it sits behind a disabled feature (`kWebContentsDiscard`), and Chrome's own discard replaces the tab's WebContents; neither is worth the risk for parity. Freezing is not used: the Electron scheduler and CONTEXT.md have no frozen state. Unloading a profile closes its pages at once, without beforeunload (ADR 0026), and a dialog left waiting in a tab that sleeps goes with its page (ADR 0074).

Protected tabs never sleep: an audible tab (`PaneWebContents.audible`, Chrome's audio indicator), one with DevTools open, and, beyond CONTEXT.md's list, one in picture-in-picture, since a floating video would otherwise vanish while it plays silently. The `sleep` scenario keeps an audible background tab loaded with a 19 kHz tone at -57 dBFS, above Chrome's -72 dBFS silence and below hearing; with the tone off, the tab sleeps and the scenario fails. Recent interaction is protected by construction, as in the Electron app: a tab's clock starts when it leaves the selection. A tab loaded in the background starts its clock at the next tick, which starts one for every loaded tab that is not active and has none. Until the review fixes of 2026-10-27 only a selection change started a clock, so a link opened in a new tab, a page's background `window.open`, or an extension's tab never slept until selected once. A hidden profile's tabs now drain first, so two unload tests turn tab sleep off, as `restartsHiddenClock` did.

Trim removes the HTTP cache, Cache Storage, and service workers, through Chrome's `BrowsingDataRemover`, as the Electron app's `clearData(cache, serviceWorkers)` did. Cookies, local storage, and IndexedDB stay. The size it checks against the budget is the HTTP cache's (`ComputeHttpCacheSize`), as Electron's `getCacheSize` was. One case is fixed from the Electron scheduler. A hidden profile's tabs are all in the background, so with tab sleep sooner than unload, as the defaults are (15 and 30 minutes), tab sleep always drained a hidden profile before its unload was due: the unload branch never ran, and the profile was never trimmed. The native scheduler treats a hidden profile whose last tabs just slept as unloaded, and trims it; two PaneKit tests cover it. Without an unload setting, nothing is trimmed, as before.

Chrome's tab discarding went off in the engine review (2026-10-28). Chrome discards a tab by swapping an empty WebContents into it, and the app's `PaneWebContents` stayed with the one destroyed: a blank tab, an empty view, and no callback again. The `DisableTabDiscarding` feature, set beside the features `engine/pane/browser/browser_application.mm` turns off, stops Chrome's urgent (memory pressure) and suggested discards. It still lets an extension discard (`chrome.tabs.discard`), even the selected tab, so the bridge hands the app's object to the new WebContents as Chrome announces it (`AboutToBeDiscarded`), before any tab strip notification, and the app's map from object to tab holds. The tab gets a new id from Chrome, as it does in Chrome itself. Reporting the discard as a removal was rejected: the tab would leave the sidebar. A discarded tab loads when the app shows it (`setVisible:YES`), where Chrome reloads one that becomes its focused window's active tab, which a Pane window that is not key never is. The selected tab loads at once, and its new view takes the old one's place in the app's panel. The `tab-discard` scenario checks it: before, selecting the discarded tab never loaded its page (no renderer, so `Page.enable` timed out); now it loads in 155 to 209 ms, and after the selected tab's discard the panel hit-tests to the reloaded page's view.
