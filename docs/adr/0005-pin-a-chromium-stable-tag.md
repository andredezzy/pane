# Pane pins a Chromium stable tag

The engine is built from one Chromium stable release, named by its tag in `engine/CHROMIUM_VERSION` (157.0.8103.15 now), and the pin moves only through the upgrade routine in `engine/UPGRADING.md`, which is how Pane takes Chromium's security fixes. The rest of the engine is made against the pin: the checkout is a clone of the tag (ADR 0007), each patch is the diff of its files against it (ADR 0008), `engine/scripts/package.sh` refuses to archive an engine that is not the pinned version, and the engine key hashes `CHROMIUM_VERSION` (ADR 0049).

The first pin, on 2026-10-26, was 156.0.8078.48, the newest Mac stable on chromiumdash that day. Ardia's patch series targets 156.0.8078.47, one patch release behind, so its patches could apply with little or no fuzz. Until the first engine build existed, Chrome for Testing 156.0.8078.48 (`~/chromium/cft`) stood in for it, only to validate the scenario runner; it was never shipped or patched.

Phase 6 moved the pin to 157.0.8103.15 the same day. Mac stable was still 156.0.8078.48, but 157.0.8103.15 had reached the stable channel in its staged rollout (Windows, 2026-10-22), so the upgrade went to it, a new milestone, rather than to a 156 patch release: the milestone is the harder case for the runbook. Any stable release's tag builds for the Mac. The routine is scripted in `engine/scripts/`: `apply-patches.sh --reverse` takes the patches out, `sync.sh` refuses to move the checkout while they are in, and `apply-patches.sh` merges a moved patch three ways and stops on conflicts; once `make-patch.sh` has regenerated the resolved patch, it continues after the patches already applied. Patches 0001–0005 applied as they were, and 0007 merged on its own. 0006 had three conflicts. Two were mechanical (a reformatted call; a new method at the end of `content_browser_client.cc`). In the third, 157 resolves `startRendering()`'s promise per rendered chunk (`OfflineAudioContext::OnChunkRendered`), before the completion event, so the audio noise moved there, ahead of both. `//pane` needed two changes: its dependency on the `//chrome/browser/ui` aggregate, which 157 split into source sets, and `BrowserWindow`'s two profile IPH methods, which 157 removed. The sync took about 25 minutes (one transient googlesource error, re-run), and the build 1h44m, about 45,600 objects at 12 jobs.

Two behaviors changed with Chrome 157, and Pane keeps both. Page fullscreen needs a focused page (`SuppressFullscreenFromUnfocusedView`, crbug.com/40052279): a page in a window that is not key is refused ("not granted"), and a user's click gives it focus. The `fullscreen` scenario had requested fullscreen from a page that had none, while another app was in use. It now does what the click does: the new automation command `activate` brings Pane forward, and then it clicks the page. 156 passed the old scenario under the same conditions and 157 refused it, so the change is Chrome's, not Pane's. A new extension is pinned to the toolbar (`kExtensionsPinnedByDefault`, on by default in 157). The Electron app showed every extension's button, so the default comes closer to it; `extension-management` expects a new extension pinned, then unpins and pins it. Running a failing scenario on the previous engine under the same conditions is how the routine tells Chrome's changes from Pane's (`engine/UPGRADING.md`, step 5).
