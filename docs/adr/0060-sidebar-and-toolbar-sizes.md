# The sidebar and toolbar take Aside's sizes with 12-point text, UI text is drawn without font smoothing, and the content panel stays Pane's

> **On the Views line** (`docs/plans/views-migration.md`, H4): the toolbar (`pane/ui/toolbar/`) keeps these sizes to the pixel against the Swift's renders: Back at 265, the address from 369 and 32 tall, its text 11 in, the domain 14 in, the divider half a point wide 12 after the domain's width rounded up to the pixel, as SwiftUI lays text out, and the chip 28 tall. Views loses the system font's kerning between a digit and the punctuation beside it, since `render_text_harfbuzz.cc` shapes them as separate runs (a digit can begin a keycap emoji): an address of digits ("127.0.0.1") is 3.5 points wider than the Swift's, a version ("v0.19.1") 1.8, and the title after it moves with it. Letters, and digits among themselves, keep their kerning. The user accepted that (2026-11-09) rather than a `ui/gfx` edit (ADR 0004's count).

> **UI text on the Views line is smoothed,** by the user's call (2026-11-09): Skia smooths CoreText's glyphs whenever the hinting isn't none, `ui/gfx/font_render_params_mac.cc` sets it to medium with no seam, and `AppleFontSmoothing` reaches no Views text, so Views' text is 10 to 18% heavier than the Swift's. The user accepted that rather than a patch to that file (ADR 0004's count). It stays grayscale rather than subpixel.

The sidebar and the toolbar take Aside's sizes, at the user's request (Aside's and Dia's font sizes "and everything else"), with their text at 12 points, the Electron app's size, by the user's call. The content panel keeps Pane's own frame, at the user's correction: the panel is Pane's, not Aside's. Aside set the numbers because its layout is Pane's, a sidebar and then a panel with the toolbar inside. Its window was captured alone, measured at 2×, and the capture deleted; Dia was not open, and was not opened. Paper took the sizes first, on every window board (ADR 0058).

**The sidebar** is 260 points wide, Aside's width, and the resizable sidebar's default (ADR 0061). Tab rows, New tab, New, and Settings are 32 points, 4 apart, on Aside's 36-point pitch; profile headers are 36. All round at 15. The favicon is 16 points, 7 in, and the title starts 10 after it, 41 points from the window's edge (Aside's: 40.5). Collapse all is a 28-point circle.

**The toolbar's** back, forward, and reload are 32-point circles. Back and forward are Aside's chevrons, 1.5 points of stroke at 24, and reload is 17 points at the same stroke. The pinned actions and the puzzle are 32-point circles too, the pinned ones in a capsule that shows only under the pointer (ADR 0054), and the profile chip is a 28-point capsule, as Aside's toolbar button is. The address at rest shows the domain (without www.), a divider, and the page's title, as Aside's does; at the sidebar's default width the domain starts 383 points from the window's edge, as Aside's. A click edits the URL: the field stays under the label, its text clear until focused, and the label lets the click through, so the field takes it as before.

**The text** is 12 points: the tab titles, New tab, New, Settings, the profile names, the sidebar's title, the address, and the profile chip. Rendered, a cap is 17 pixels at 2×. Paper follows: 1,633 texts, the type scale, and the captions. The size came down in steps, the rows keeping Aside's spacing throughout. Taking Aside's sizes set it at 14, as Aside's sidebar measured (a 10-point cap height; a 14-point title's caps rendered 20 pixels at 2×, as Aside's), and the user found it far too large. That is a point over macOS's own sidebar size, since Finder's, Safari's, and Mail's sidebars and the menu bar are 13, so it went to 13: a cap of 18 pixels at 2×, against the menu bar's 19 in the user's screenshot. After 13, the user's call was 12.

**UI text is drawn without font smoothing**, as the Electron app's UI was: `AppleFontSmoothing` 0 in the defaults the app registers (`app/Pane/main.swift`), the counterpart of the Electron UI's `-webkit-font-smoothing: antialiased`. With smoothing on, SwiftUI text was visibly heavier. Web pages render the same either way, since Chromium draws them.

**The content panel** was first made flush with the window, as Aside's is, with the traffic lights on the toolbar's line and a hairline under the toolbar. All of that was reverted. The panel keeps its 8-point gutters, its radius of 18, and its ring, and the traffic lights stay at (12, 12) (ADR 0036).
