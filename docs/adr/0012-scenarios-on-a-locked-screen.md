# Scenarios run unattended on the user's Mac: they never reach its apps or its login keychain or take its front, clean up after themselves, wait for conditions, and report what they could not prove

> **On the Views line** (`docs/plans/views-migration.md`, H1): on this Mac (macOS 27), AppKit makes a copy the runner starts from the app in front active as it finishes launching, the published 0.19.2 as much as the Views build, and it kept the front for the whole run. A scenario's Views copy without `--foreground` now hands it back at once to the app it took it from (`pane/ui/app/app_events_mac.mm`): the user's app stayed in front for every 50 ms sample of a launch. The Swift app on `swift` still takes it.

> **On the Views line** (`docs/plans/views-migration.md`, H3): a press handed to the widget takes the mouse whenever the user's own button happens to be down (`Widget::OnMouseEvent` reads `[NSEvent pressedMouseButtons]`), and the copy then hears the user's release elsewhere, which ended 3 of 120 stress drags; `pointerWindow` and `dragWindow` hand the press, the moves, and the release to the root view, and only the pointer's move to the press to the widget.

> **On the Views line** (`docs/plans/views-migration.md`, H7): a key posted into the app's queue reaches the menu bar without the front, as the Swift's did, so ⌘T, ⌘Q, and Ctrl-Tab run in the background; a key the window itself takes (typing, Esc, Ctrl's release) needs the key window, so without the front a scenario sends it through Views' own dispatch (`dispatchKey`), and a check of a key queued behind another defers.

> **On the Views line** (`docs/plans/views-migration.md`, K1): a scenario's copy that may not take the front opens the new Pane after Restart to update behind the app in use (`open -g`), where the Swift's took the front with it.

> **On the Views line** (`docs/plans/views-migration.md`, What Chrome asks for): a page's dialog is answered by a real click on its button through the channel (`clickSheetButton`), its fields filled first as a value set through accessibility is, with no cua-driver or accessibility tree of the window; its Esc and Return go through Views' own dispatch without the front (`pressWindowKey`, `tests/lib/pane.ts`); the system's file panel, Chrome's stock one run out of process, is answered beside it as the Swift's was, the channel's own listener between the panel and Chrome's (`pane/automation/file_panels.h`), and the panel's accessory view, a window macOS hosts beside it, counts as the sheet's.

> **On the Views line** (`docs/plans/views-migration.md`, X3 to X5's runs): on an unlocked screen too, a background test window can be occluded: the user's windows cover most of it and the system's file panel, out of process, the rest. Chromium then hides the page, shows it no more once the panel goes, and Chrome's `FileSelectHelper` ends the next chooser of a page that isn't visible (`OnVisibilityChanged`), so `file-chooser` failed 4 of 4 on 2026-11-11 and passed twice with `--disable-backgrounding-occluded-windows`, which it now passes always, its subject being the panels. `views-dialogs` failed the same way in dark, an external app's prompt never asked for (five runs in three hours), and passed twice with it, so every background run now passes it (`launchPane`), as a locked one did: a background window is the user's windows' to cover, and a page hidden by them is no subject of any scenario; one in front runs with Chromium's own occlusion, as the user sees it.

> **On the Views line** (`docs/plans/views-migration.md`, K2a): the launch opens the user's data, where the Views build had refused every launch but a test copy while it was built, and its guard is turned around (`pane/app/test_copy.h`). A launch whose data lies in the temporary folder, by `PANE_DATA_DIR` or under a home of its own, is a test copy and runs on the mock keychain alone; started without it, as 0.19.1's installer relaunches a scenario's copy (its `PANE_` variables and home, no switch), it exits with 1 before it reads anything, so no test copy reaches the user's Keychain item. The mock keychain runs a test copy alone: on data outside the temporary folder it is refused too, since its fixed secret would seal a key where the user's data has none yet, and a scenario's hooks would reach the user's data. Every other launch is the user's, and opens their data as the Swift's did; a test copy still reads and writes nothing outside the temporary folder (`PrepareData`). `views-start` proves both refusals without the switch, by `PANE_DATA_DIR` and by a home alone, under a sandbox that denies the Keychain's services and files, the mock keychain's refusal outside the temporary folder, and the test path; `pane_unittests` the rule, link by link (`LaunchKindTest`).

> **On the Views line** (`docs/plans/views-migration.md`, D1 and K3): CI runs no scenario since the App workflow went (ADR 0049), so a run takes the front only through `run.ts --foreground`, on a Mac whose user allowed it or that is idle; every scenario runs on this Mac before a push or a tag (AGENTS.md).

Scenarios run on the Mac the user works on, often unattended and on a locked screen, so the runner (`tests/run.ts`) leaves the user's apps, front, and files as it found them, has each check wait for its condition rather than a fixed time, and reports each check it could not make: a partial pass is never taken for a full one, and a failure is the app's, not the runner's. The rules came from incidents and a review of the runner (2026-10-27).

**What a run reports.** Each entry records `locked` (the screen was locked at its start or end) and `deferred`, the checks the scenario skipped (`context.defer`). `report.json` is written however a scenario ends, and its `complete` is true only when every scenario passed and nothing was deferred. A scenario defers each check that only the screen, or Pane in front, can prove. A check that runs another way on a locked screen is told by `locked`.

**A locked screen.** A locked session hides every window from accessibility and refuses input, so a window screenshot, an accessibility read, and a real ⌘Q cannot be proven under it. Scenarios prove what they can another way:

- Locked, macOS reports the window occluded, and Chromium hides its pages once occlusion tracking is live (after a page's first real show, ADR 0011): no visual updates, and no hang monitor. So while the screen is locked, `launchPane` passes `--disable-backgrounding-occluded-windows`, Chromium's switch for this exact case.
- A dialog is answered with a CUA click on an unlocked screen, else through the automation channel (ADR 0018), which presses the same button a click would. macOS 27 runs open and save panels out of process (`-[NSSavePanel ok:]` throws "not implemented"), so a locked run answers a file chooser beside its panel.
- Chromium ignores an unresponsive page while a debugger is attached, and mouse input never arms its hang monitor (mouse events are non-blocking), so `hung-renderer` attaches no CDP and types a key into the page.
- While a page is hidden, a zoom change shows in the engine's zoom level but not in the page's `devicePixelRatio`, so `shortcuts` asserts the engine level always and the page ratio whenever the page is visible, and re-runs on an unlocked screen.
- The switcher's picture under the lock is an offscreen render of the app's views (`switcher.png`, from `shortcuts`), which shows the app's UI but not page pixels.

A page left blank under the lock is the lock's artifact, not Pane's. Under it the window shows the page's background color but not its content, while stock Chrome's views windows paint, and a content_shell build, Chromium's reference embedder on the same standalone compositor path, shows only the background color under the same lock too. `hosted` passes locked on what it can prove: the page `visible`, laid out at the content area's size (1060×820), and composited by the engine (a CDP capture of it showed the full page); `WebContentsViewCocoa` unhidden with Chromium's `CALayerHost` attached; and no helper left after a graceful shutdown. It re-checks everything, the visual parts included, on an unlocked screen.

CUA reads a page only with full accessibility on. Chrome builds a page's accessibility tree, with its actions, once an assistive app turns it on: VoiceOver asks the app for its role and sets `AXEnhancedUserInterface`, and Chrome waits two quiet seconds. cua-driver does neither, so scenarios do it (`tests/tools/ax-activate.swift`) before clicking a page's button, such as the Web Store's install button; the click is then a real, trusted press with user activation.

**Never the front of the app in use** (2026-10-30). Eight scenarios brought the app in front with `activate(ignoringOtherApps:)`, so whoever worked on the Mac lost their keys to a test Pane mid-run; even the cooperative `activate()` took the front, for 3.4 s of one run while the user was in another app. The automation's `activate` now takes the front only when the runner allows it (`run.ts --foreground`, and CI, where no one works; `PANE_AUTOMATION_FOREGROUND`), and otherwise brings the window forward without the app. What needs Pane in front (keys a panel takes, a page's fullscreen, Quit Pane?) defers through `bringToFront` (`tests/lib/pane.ts`): `quit`, `key-precedence`, and `fullscreen` whole, `security`'s ⌘Q with Caps Lock, and `tabs-api`'s `chrome.action.openPopup`. Without the flag the eight passed with those deferred, and the user's app stayed in front for all 720 samples of the 72 s.

**Never the user's apps** (2026-10-26). `external-protocol` first used a `mailto:` link, which Chrome opens without asking, by design, so macOS opened the user's Mail with an unsent compose window. It was quit at once, and nothing was sent. A scenario that opens another app's link now uses a scheme Chrome always asks about: `external-protocol` uses `maps:`, answers Cancel, and checks that Maps did not open; its fixed wait is there to prove that nothing opens. `docs/human-checklist.md` asks the user to look for a stray draft.

**Never the login keychain.** Every scenario launch passes `--use-mock-keychain` (`tests/lib/browser.ts`), so a run never reads or writes the user's login Keychain: a freshly signed build asked for the keychain password for its Safe Storage item ("Chromium Safe Storage" then) on every launch. A Keychain item's access list names the app by its designated requirement, and an ad-hoc signature's is its code hash, which every build changes; only a Developer ID ends those prompts for the shipped app (ADR 0048). The Keychain also failed the first runs under a locked session: the ad-hoc signed app could neither read nor create its item (`-25320`), so Chrome reported "Encryption is not available" and kept cookies unencrypted for that run. Stock Chrome from the same build shows the same message. The shipped app's item is "Pane Safe Storage", account "Pane" (patch 0001), the item the migration fills with the Electron app's key (ADR 0043).

The app's own Keychain use honors the same switch, so scenarios never touch the login Keychain at all. Under `--use-mock-keychain` the data key is sealed under a fixed secret, not the engine's Keychain item (`SafeStorage.dataSecret`, ADR 0113), so a scenario's data opens with none of the user's, and the proxy passwords an earlier Pane kept apart are read from `mock-keychain.json` in the data directory, not the "Pane Proxy Passwords" item (ADR 0037). Only under the switch does the app take its test hooks (`TestHooks`, ADR 0115). What only the real Keychain shows stays unproven by a scenario: the data key sealed under the engine's secret (ADR 0113), and the migration's adoption of the Electron app's cookie key, since a folder named by `PANE_ELECTRON_DATA_DIR` has no app name, so no key is adopted (ADR 0043).

**The machine as it was.** A scenario owns its temp dirs (`context.tempDir(prefix)`), `launchPane`'s data dir among them. When it ends, pass or fail, the runner detaches the encrypted volumes attached from them (`detachImagesIn`, ADR 0113), kills whatever still uses them, found by the dir in a process's arguments (`tests/lib/processes.ts`), and removes them. The data dir had leaked on every launch, 11 GB in `$TMPDIR`; in a private `$TMPDIR`, six scenarios (`smoke quit security restore profile-lifecycle downloads`) now leave nothing. A failed launch kills its app and removes its dirs, and an app that exits before serving DevTools fails it at once: `/usr/bin/false` takes 261 ms to fail, not 60 s. The CDP client rejects pending calls and waiters once its socket closes, and `Browser.close` is skipped for an app that already quit: `quit`'s teardown takes 85 ms, not a 15 s timeout. The teardown also stops the panic wipe's new instance (`security`), which is no child of the runner and had been stopped only if every check before it passed.

**Deadlines.** Each scenario has one: `--timeout`, 600 s by default. A scenario past it fails, its apps and dirs go, and the run goes on. Under `--timeout 3`, a scenario that never returns failed at 3,002 ms, and the next one ran. Rejections a scenario leaves behind are logged, not fatal.

**One copy of each helper.** `tests/lib/targets.ts` finds DevTools targets, in place of five hand-written loops; `waitForExit`, and `launchTraced` with `waitForEntry`, replace hand-parsed traces; there is one `windowsOf`. `tests/lib/tools.ts` builds every Swift tool, and builds it again once its source is newer: an edited tool used to keep running its old build from `$TMPDIR`.

**Conditions, not fixed times.** A check waits for what it needs, with `tests/lib/poll.ts` and `PaneApp.settled`. `ubol` loads its page again until the rules block the ads, `sidebar-reorder` reads the order once the drop has changed it, and `hung-renderer` types once the page names itself Hanging. `password-managers` presses its button once no more tabs open (2 s quiet), and branches on what the press opened, since 1Password turns its popup off seconds after installing. Installing from the Web Store clicks its button again, every 3 s, until the install sheet comes: the store wires the button a moment after it shows, and the one click, made as soon as it showed, was lost, so `dark-reader`, `password-managers`, and `ubol` waited out a minute and failed on every run. `sidebar-scroll` reads the rows' frames once the list shows the row and holds still, two reads alike. The fixed waits that stay prove that something does not happen (`quiet`, `migration`, `external-protocol`, `dialog-beforeunload`, `password-manager-off`, `extensions-per-profile`, `window-chrome`, `sleep`), or let an animation finish before an evidence capture. Three wait for something no signal shows: the spellchecker's verdict (`context-menu`), and the key-status flicker a new tab or a closed popup causes (`extensions-menu`).

**Controls as they are now.** A scenario reads a control again before it acts on it. `extensions-menu` reopens the more menu before pressing a row: it had failed twice when the window gave up key status mid-run (an extension's tab or popup taking it while the app is not frontmost), which closes the menu, as it should. `clickControl` reads an accessibility element again and presses, up to three times, when AppKit refuses one SwiftUI has replaced since the read (kAXErrorInvalidUIElement, -25202), and for that error alone. A scenario also clicks where the controls are now: when the sidebar widened to Aside's 260 points (ADR 0060), `window-chrome`'s click at the old back position landed on Collapse all. It clicks back at (281, 29) and Collapse all at (236, 19.5).

**The app's own clock.** A check times from the moment the app's clock starts. `sleep` timed a background tab's sleep from the last tab's opening, most of a second after the tab before it had started the clock by taking the selection, so the check passed or failed on where the scheduler's one-second ticks fell, from build to build.
