# VoiceOver and Narrator name Pane's controls, read their text, and stay in the modal layer on top

Pane's own controls read to VoiceOver by what they are and what they hold, not by their placeholders or by the text drawn over them, through Views' accessibility (`GetViewAccessibility()`), and VoiceOver reads only the layer of the window on top while a modal one shows. On Windows Narrator reads the same through UI Automation, Chromium's own mapping of Views' data; where the two read the same data apart, each system's half of `pane/ui/design/screen_reader.h` gives its own reader what it reads right (W8c, below).

**What each control reads.**

- **The sidebar** (`pane/ui/sidebar/`): a profile's row keeps its name as its accessible name and reads its state as its value ("3 tabs, collapsed, proxy connected", `profile_row.cc`), selected while its page shows; a tab's row reads its title, Loading without one, selected while shown, and its close button Close tab; the sidebar's edge is a splitter, Sidebar width, valued at the width and stepping by 10.
- **The toolbar** (`pane/ui/toolbar/`): the address's name sits on the field itself, and the domain and title drawn over it at rest read as text of their own: set on the whole view, the name renamed them (ADR 0060). Reload reads Stop while the page loads. The update pill's button reads its status, and its dismiss button Dismiss.
- **A page's dialog** reads as a modal dialog by its title, its fields by their placeholders (Text without one, the password's protected), and its buttons by their titles, the default marked (ADR 0021), on Windows' card as on the Mac's sheet (ADR 0126). An extension's prompt reads the same way, its accept marked the default, and Report abuse as a checkbox, checked or not (ADR 0056).
- **Pane's own alert and About Pane on Windows** (ADR 0126; Paper 11.13 A, 11.15 A) read as modal dialogs, the alert by its title ("Delete “Work”?"), About as "About Pane", their words read in them, which hold the keys, and their buttons by their titles; a screen reader stays in them while they show, as in Exit Pane? (`pane.alert`, `pane.about`).
- **Find**: its field reads "Find in page" though it shows no placeholder (ADR 0067), its count its text, and its buttons Previous match, Next match, and Close find bar. Docked DevTools read "Developer tools", the page "Web content".
- **The extensions** (ADR 0054): the puzzle reads Extensions, selected while its menu shows; a pinned action its action's title, else its name, selected while its popup shows; the menu reads as the group Extensions, Installed extensions as a heading, each row by its title, a pin Pin to toolbar or Unpin from toolbar, selected while pinned, and More; the more menu as a group named by its extension. Chrome's extensions pages read as Chrome's.
- **The profile sheet** (ADR 0120) reads as a modal dialog, New profile or Edit profile, its controls by their labels in its focus order, with the identifiers the Swift app's had (`pane.profile.*`, `pane/ui/view_ids.cc`): the proxy's fields Proxy host and Proxy port, each switch by its title with its sentence as its description, the platform's tiles by their platform, selected when chosen. A problem is its field's description, announced when Save is refused.
- **The PIN screen** (ADR 0042) reads as a modal dialog (`pane.lockScreen`): its keys by their digits and Delete, the dots as "2 of 4 digits", the back arrow Back, Continue by its title, the attempts left as text, the data's move as "Encrypting your data" with its percent as its value, and the update's action by its title. A wrong PIN is announced with the attempts left.
- **The pickers** (`MenuButton`) read their label as their name and their option as their value ("Theme, System"), "menu pop up pop up button", as Chromium's own menu buttons do.

**Text under a view that holds the keys is exposed.** VoiceOver found what the names alone didn't show, in the front session with VoiceOver of 2026-11-12. Outside ChromeOS, Chromium leaves out every view that can't take the focus under one that can (`ViewAccessibility::UpdateIgnoredState`), and the PIN screen, Settings, the dialogs, an extension's prompt, and the sad tab hold the keys that way, so their text went: the PIN screen's dots, its error, and the attempts left, Settings' headings and descriptions, a page's `alert()` message, Quit Pane?'s, a prompt's permissions; a wrong PIN said nothing. Such text now takes the focus in accessibility mode alone (`ExposeToScreenReaders`, `pane/ui/design/text.h`), as Chromium's own dialogs expose their body text, and Settings' section labels read as headings. The sidebar's edge takes the focus in accessibility mode alone too, a splitter having its value only while focusable; VoiceOver still reads it "horizontal … collapsed on top", from an orientation and adjoining contents Chromium's Mac nodes for views don't expose. The sheet's time zone reads once, its note as its description.

**VoiceOver stays in the modal layer on top.** Under the PIN screen, a sheet, or a dialog, VoiceOver walked the rest of the window, and opened Settings' menus over the Lock (a choice made there was refused): Views leaves the rest of a window out only for a modal child window, not for a modal view in it. The root view leaves out each layer under the topmost modal one that shows, and each hidden one (`UpdateAccessibleLayers`, `pane/ui/design/accessible_layers.h`), which also ends a control of hidden Settings read beside the page (Chromium counts a view shown inside a hidden one as shown to its own children) and the hidden page under Settings. The Swift app kept VoiceOver on the PIN screen with SwiftUI's `.isModal`, and named its sheet's fields through a `LabeledField` of its own.

**On Windows, Narrator** (W8c, `evidence/views-migration/runs/p5-w8c-notes.md`): every surface was walked with Narrator in the test VM on an installed Pane, its words taken from its own Speech recap, and read through UI Automation as Narrator walks it. What Narrator was heard to read right of ARIA's roles in Pane's own pages chose Windows' data, since Pane's views reach UI Automation by the same mapping; the Mac's data stayed as it was.

- **Where the two read apart** (`screen_reader.h`, `screen_reader_mac.cc`, `screen_reader_win.cc`, ADR 0124's platform files): UI Automation gives no button a selection or a value but a pop-up's, a heading no "heading" without a level, a focusable splitter is a thumb without its value, and Narrator walks past a group with nothing in it and cannot leave text with no text of its own. So on Windows the sidebar is a tree, as Explorer's panes are: a profile an item of its first level, expanded or collapsed, named with its tabs and proxy ("Work, 2 tabs", as Outlook's folders read their unread count, a tree item having no value and Narrator reading no tree item's description), a tab one of its second, each at its place and selected while it shows ("Work, 2 tabs, 1 of 2 level 1, collapsed, non-selected", "127.0.0.1, 2 of 2 level 2, selected"); a picker a combo box valued at its option ("Theme, System, combo box, collapsed, has popup"); a profile's colors and platforms, and the App icon's skies, radio buttons at their places, the chosen one selected ("Blue, radio button, selected, 1 of 9"); the Settings button a toggle, on while Settings shows; each heading at its level, a dialog's and the sheet's title 1, Settings' sections and the extensions menu's 2 ("Appearance heading level 2"); the PIN's dots an image ("0 of 4 digits, image"); the sidebar's edge a slider ("Sidebar width, slider at 260"); the puzzle has its menu and is expanded or collapsed, as Chrome's, and an action is expanded while its popup shows. VoiceOver reads all of it as before.
- **Where both read it wrong, fixed for both:** Views' `Button::SetState` sets a button's checked state at each change of its own state (hovered, pressed, normal), so a switch or a checkbox read off once the pointer left it; Pane's `Switch` and `Checkbox`, and the choices and the Settings button, keep their own (`UpdateAccessibleCheckedState`). An extension's prompt could not take the focus under the overlay holding the keys, and Chromium left it out, its title with it (Narrator: "region"): the overlay is the dialog, by the prompt's title. Quit Pane? (Exit Pane?) and the App icon dialog are named by their titles. A hidden `Reveal` of the profile sheet leaves accessibility as a leaf, its subtree with it (`LeaveOutOfAccessibility`, the layers' own way): Views still gave the proxy's fields, hidden in it, to UI Automation. What opens a menu or a popup takes Expand and Collapse as a press (`screen_reader::PressToExpand`), as Chrome's app menu button does: Chromium gives what expands Expand in Invoke's place, and Views acts on neither, so UI Automation's Expand, which Narrator's primary action asks of what expands, was refused on "Pane ⌄" (the runner's press); since, Narrator's primary action opens its menu.
- **A wrong PIN is said wherever nothing else says it** ("Wrong PIN", an alert, once the shake's lines are in: `lock_screen_view.cc`): at Windows' Lock, which shows no attempts line (07.1.1), and in Change and Remove PIN on both systems; the Mac's Lock says the attempts left with the shake, as before.
- **On Windows alone:** the installer's and the uninstall's windows read as dialogs, and their message is said as a notification a moment after a screen reader first reaches the window and at each new screen (`pane/installer/uia_element.h`): Narrator says a dialog's name and kind, never its full description.
- **What Windows has no word for:** UI Automation has no default button, so Narrator says none of a dialog's (VoiceOver: "default button"); Narrator speaks the PIN's digits as they are typed, the focus being the PIN screen's panel, not a password field.

How it is checked:

- `pane_unittests`: `AccessibleLayersTest` (a modal layer leaves out what is under it; a hidden layer stays out with what shows in it) and `ScreenReaderTextTest` (text under a view holding the keys is read once exposed).
- `profile-sheet` reads a profile row's name and value, the sheet's role, name, and modality, and its controls' names and selection from Views' accessibility; it failed before. On Windows `profile-lifecycle` reads Delete profile's alert, and `about` About Pane, the same way.
- The rigs read the rest through the channel (`dialogAccessibility`, `findAccessibility`, `extensionsAccessibility`, `accessibility`): `tests/rigs/views-browsing.ts`, `views-dialogs.ts`, `views-extensions.ts`, `views-keys.ts`, and `views-profile-sheet.ts`; `views-lock.ts` reads the system's accessibility tree at the Lock.
- VoiceOver itself, through its AppleScript, walked the window, the Lock, the sheet, and the dialogs on 2026-11-12 (`docs/human-checklist.md`).
- `screen-reader` reads each system's own tree (the Mac's AX tree, Windows' UI Automation control view, `tests/lib/windows.ts`): the sidebar's rows and their places, the shown tab, the edge's width, the Settings button, Settings' headings and a picker's choice, the App icon dialog and its chosen sky, the sheet's chosen color, the proxy's fields only once it is on and its switch on still once the pointer has left it, Quit Pane? and an extension's prompt by their titles, and the PIN's dots. `profile-sheet` reads a choice checked and the row named with its tabs on Windows.
- Narrator itself walked every surface in the test VM on 2026-11-16 (W8c).
