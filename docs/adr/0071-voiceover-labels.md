# VoiceOver and Narrator name Pane's controls, read their text, and stay in the modal layer on top

Pane's own controls read to VoiceOver by what they are and what they hold, not by their placeholders or by the text drawn over them, through Views' accessibility (`GetViewAccessibility()`), and VoiceOver reads only the layer of the window on top while a modal one shows. On Windows Narrator reads the same through UI Automation, Chromium's own mapping of Views' data; where the two read the same data apart, each system's half of `pane/ui/design/screen_reader.h` gives its own reader what it reads right (W8c, below).

**What each control reads.**

- **The sidebar** (`pane/ui/sidebar/`): a profile's row keeps its name as its accessible name and reads its state as its value ("3 tabs, collapsed, proxy connected", `profile_row.cc`), selected while its page shows; a tab's row reads its title, Loading without one, selected while shown, and its close button Close tab; the sidebar's edge is a splitter, Sidebar width, valued at the width, which it says as it changes, and stepping by 20, a tenth of its range, as Views' sliders step: by an assistive app's increment, and by its arrows while it has the keys (Right and Up wider, Left and Down narrower), which a screen reader's focus gives it (W8d, below).
- **The toolbar** (`pane/ui/toolbar/`): the address's name sits on the field itself, and the domain and title drawn over it at rest read as text of their own: set on the whole view, the name renamed them (ADR 0060). Reload reads Stop while the page loads. The update pill's button reads its status, and its dismiss button Dismiss.
- **A page's dialog** reads as a modal dialog by its title, its fields by their placeholders (Text without one, the password's protected), and its buttons by their titles, the default marked (ADR 0021), on Windows' card as on the Mac's sheet (ADR 0126). An extension's prompt reads the same way, its accept marked the default, and Report abuse as a checkbox, checked or not (ADR 0056).
- **Pane's own alert and About Pane on Windows** (ADR 0126; Paper 11.13 A, 11.15 A) read as modal dialogs, the alert by its title ("Delete “Work”?"), About as "About Pane", their words read in them, which hold the keys, and their buttons by their titles; a screen reader stays in them while they show, as in Exit Pane? (`pane.alert`, `pane.about`).
- **Find**: its field reads "Find in page" though it shows no placeholder (ADR 0067), its count its text, and its buttons Previous match, Next match, and Close find bar. Docked DevTools read "Developer tools", the page "Web content".
- **The extensions** (ADR 0054): the puzzle reads Extensions, selected while its menu shows; a pinned action its action's title, else its name, selected while its popup shows; the menu reads as the group Extensions, and says "Extensions menu" as it opens, Installed extensions as a heading, each row by its title, a pin Pin to toolbar or Unpin from toolbar, selected while pinned, and More; the more menu as a group named by its extension. Chrome's extensions pages read as Chrome's.
- **The profile sheet** (ADR 0120) reads as a modal dialog, New profile or Edit profile, its controls by their labels in its focus order, with the identifiers the Swift app's had (`pane.profile.*`, `pane/ui/view_ids.cc`): the proxy's fields Proxy host and Proxy port, each switch by its title with its sentence as its description, the platform's tiles by their platform, selected when chosen. A problem is its field's description, announced when Save is refused.
- **The PIN screen** (ADR 0042) reads as a modal dialog (`pane.lockScreen`) named by its step's title ("Enter new PIN", "Confirm PIN", "Enter current PIN"), each said as it shows, and "Pane is locked" at the Lock, which shows no title. What is typed goes to a password field named PIN, so no digit is read (W8d, below). Its numpad's keys read by their digits and Delete, the dots as "2 of 4 digits" (on Windows the field's description), the back arrow Back, Continue by its title, the attempts left as text, the data's move as "Encrypting your data" with its percent as its value, and the update's action by its title. A wrong PIN is announced with the attempts left. A new PIN the disk didn't save (ADR 0113, Paper 07.12; W8e) says its lines as they show, an alert, and again each time a write of it fails, Try again's own included, whose failure changes nothing on screen; its two buttons read by their titles (Keep earlier PIN, Keep PIN, or Cancel, and Try again). The chip refusing in Settings (Paper 07.13 A) says its lines with the shake, as a lockout's do.
- **The pickers** (`MenuButton`) read their label as their name and their option as their value ("Theme, System"), "menu pop up pop up button", as Chromium's own menu buttons do.

**Text under a view that holds the keys is exposed.** VoiceOver found what the names alone didn't show, in the front session with VoiceOver of 2026-11-12. Outside ChromeOS, Chromium leaves out every view that can't take the focus under one that can (`ViewAccessibility::UpdateIgnoredState`), and the PIN screen, Settings, the dialogs, an extension's prompt, and the sad tab hold the keys that way, so their text went: the PIN screen's dots, its error, and the attempts left, Settings' headings and descriptions, a page's `alert()` message, Quit Pane?'s, a prompt's permissions; a wrong PIN said nothing. Such text now takes the focus in accessibility mode alone (`ExposeToScreenReaders`, `pane/ui/design/text.h`), as Chromium's own dialogs expose their body text, and Settings' section labels read as headings. The sidebar's edge takes the focus in accessibility mode alone too, a splitter having its value only while focusable; VoiceOver still reads it "horizontal … collapsed on top", from an orientation and adjoining contents Chromium's Mac nodes for views don't expose. The sheet's time zone reads once, its note as its description.

**VoiceOver stays in the modal layer on top.** Under the PIN screen, a sheet, or a dialog, VoiceOver walked the rest of the window, and opened Settings' menus over the Lock (a choice made there was refused): Views leaves the rest of a window out only for a modal child window, not for a modal view in it. The root view leaves out each layer under the topmost modal one that shows, and each hidden one (`UpdateAccessibleLayers`, `pane/ui/design/accessible_layers.h`), which also ends a control of hidden Settings read beside the page (Chromium counts a view shown inside a hidden one as shown to its own children) and the hidden page under Settings. The Swift app kept VoiceOver on the PIN screen with SwiftUI's `.isModal`, and named its sheet's fields through a `LabeledField` of its own.

**On Windows, Narrator** (W8c, `evidence/views-migration/runs/p5-w8c-notes.md`): every surface was walked with Narrator in the test VM on an installed Pane, its words taken from its own Speech recap, and read through UI Automation as Narrator walks it. What Narrator was heard to read right of ARIA's roles in Pane's own pages chose Windows' data, since Pane's views reach UI Automation by the same mapping; the Mac's data stayed as it was.

- **Where the two read apart** (`screen_reader.h`, `screen_reader_mac.cc`, `screen_reader_win.cc`, ADR 0124's platform files): UI Automation gives no button a selection or a value but a pop-up's, a heading no "heading" without a level, a focusable splitter is a thumb without its value, and Narrator walks past a group with nothing in it and cannot leave text with no text of its own. So on Windows the sidebar is a tree, as Explorer's panes are: a profile an item of its first level, expanded or collapsed, named with its tabs and proxy ("Work, 2 tabs", as Outlook's folders read their unread count, a tree item having no value and Narrator reading no tree item's description), a tab one of its second, each at its place and selected while it shows ("Work, 2 tabs, 1 of 2 level 1, collapsed, non-selected", "127.0.0.1, 2 of 2 level 2, selected"); a picker a combo box valued at its option ("Theme, System, combo box, collapsed, has popup"); a profile's colors and platforms, and the App icon's skies, radio buttons at their places, the chosen one selected ("Blue, radio button, selected, 1 of 9"); the Settings button a toggle, on while Settings shows; each heading at its level, a dialog's and the sheet's title 1, Settings' sections and the extensions menu's 2 ("Appearance heading level 2"); the PIN's count the field's description, the dots left out ("PIN, edit, 0 of 4 digits", W8d: as an image, "0 of 4 digits, image", Narrator read on to them as the Lock came); the sidebar's edge a slider ("Sidebar width, slider at 260"); the puzzle has its menu and is expanded or collapsed, as Chrome's, and an action is expanded while its popup shows. VoiceOver reads all of it as before.
- **Where both read it wrong, fixed for both:** Views' `Button::SetState` sets a button's checked state at each change of its own state (hovered, pressed, normal), so a switch or a checkbox read off once the pointer left it; Pane's `Switch` and `Checkbox`, and the choices and the Settings button, keep their own (`UpdateAccessibleCheckedState`). An extension's prompt could not take the focus under the overlay holding the keys, and Chromium left it out, its title with it (Narrator: "region"): the overlay is the dialog, by the prompt's title. Quit Pane? (Exit Pane?) and the App icon dialog are named by their titles. A hidden `Reveal` of the profile sheet leaves accessibility as a leaf, its subtree with it (`LeaveOutOfAccessibility`, the layers' own way): Views still gave the proxy's fields, hidden in it, to UI Automation. What opens a menu or a popup takes Expand and Collapse as a press (`screen_reader::PressToExpand`), as Chrome's app menu button does: Chromium gives what expands Expand in Invoke's place, and Views acts on neither, so UI Automation's Expand, which Narrator's primary action asks of what expands, was refused on "Pane ⌄" (the runner's press); since, Narrator's primary action opens its menu.
- **A wrong PIN is said wherever nothing else says it** ("Wrong PIN", an alert, once the shake's lines are in: `lock_screen_view.cc`): at Windows' Lock, which shows no attempts line (07.1.1), and in Change and Remove PIN on both systems; the Mac's Lock says the attempts left with the shake, as before.
- **On Windows alone:** the installer's and the uninstall's windows read as dialogs, and their message is said as a notification a moment after a screen reader first reaches the window and at each new screen (`pane/installer/uia_element.h`): Narrator says a dialog's name and kind, never its full description.
- **What Windows has no word for:** UI Automation has no default button, so Narrator says none of a dialog's (VoiceOver: "default button"). Until W8d Narrator spoke the PIN's digits as they were typed, the focus being the PIN screen's panel, not a password field; now the field is a password field, and Narrator says "Hidden" for its keys at times and the keys themselves at others, with the same UI Automation events, as it does in Windows' own password box and Chrome's (below).

**W8c's follow-ups** (W8d, 2026-11-16, `evidence/views-migration/runs/p5-w8d-notes.md`), each heard in the test VM with Narrator's Speech recap and read in the Mac's AX tree:

- **The PIN is a password field on both systems:** the PIN screen's keys go to Chromium's own password `Textfield`, named PIN and drawing nothing, over the dots (`PinField`, `pane/ui/design/pin_field.h`), its text a bullet per digit typed, so a screen reader reads it as a protected field and never its digits (the Mac's AX tree: `AXTextField`, `AXSecureTextField`, "PIN"; UI Automation: an Edit, "PIN", a password; Narrator: "Pane is locked dialog, PIN, edit, 0 of 4 digits"). A view claiming a text field's role, without what Chromium's Textfield gives (editable, a leaf, its selection), left Narrator silent at the Lock. Its own key handlers being final, the field hands its keys to the PIN screen through its controller (`HandleKeyEvent`), and inserts and edits nothing; it offers no menu, which the menu key, Shift+F10, and a screen reader's Show menu open on the focused view (a text field's Paste would have shown over the Lock).
- **Narrator's echo of a password field's keys is Narrator's:** it says "Hidden" for them at times and the keys themselves at others, with the same UI Automation events (the focus on the field, a bullet more per digit), and it spoke the keys typed in Windows' own password box (a WinForms TextBox with the system's password character), in Chrome 157's own Views password field, and in a web page's password field in Chrome, in the same VM. No field controls it: the user's call.
- **The PIN screen's dialog is named by its step**, "Pane is locked" at the Lock (a name of Pane's own: the Lock shows no title), and a new step's title is said as it shows, half a second on (`AnnouncePolitely`), after the last digit's echo, under which Narrator dropped it ("Enter new PIN,").
- **The extensions menu says "Extensions menu" as it opens**, the keys staying where they were (`RootView::ShowExtensionsMenu`).
- **The sidebar's edge resizes by its arrows** on both systems, and takes a screen reader's focus where the keyboard has no accessibility mode (Windows), so Narrator's cursor brings the keys to it ("Sidebar width, slider at 280," then "300", "320", "300").
- **Exit Pane? and an extension's prompt take Space or Enter on the button with the ring on Windows** (ADR 0126): Enter on a focused Cancel had accepted both; the Mac keeps Return for the default button.
- **"New line" between typed letters is Chromium's with Narrator, not Pane's:** each sign-in dialog's Username focused, then "Work" typed 6 s on, Narrator said "cap W, o, new line, r, k" in Pane's and in Chrome 157's own alike, and UI Automation saw a value change per letter and nothing else in Pane's.

How it is checked:

- `pane_unittests`: `AccessibleLayersTest` (a modal layer leaves out what is under it; a hidden layer stays out with what shows in it), `ScreenReaderTextTest` (text under a view holding the keys is read once exposed), and `PinFieldTest` (the PIN's field a password field named PIN, valued at a bullet per digit, with no menu, editing nothing, its keys going to the PIN screen).
- `profile-sheet` reads a profile row's name and value, the sheet's role, name, and modality, and its controls' names and selection from Views' accessibility; it failed before. On Windows `profile-lifecycle` reads Delete profile's alert, and `about` About Pane, the same way.
- The rigs read the rest through the channel (`dialogAccessibility`, `findAccessibility`, `extensionsAccessibility`, `accessibility`): `tests/rigs/views-browsing.ts`, `views-dialogs.ts`, `views-extensions.ts`, `views-keys.ts`, and `views-profile-sheet.ts`; `views-lock.ts` reads the system's accessibility tree at the Lock.
- VoiceOver itself, through its AppleScript, walked the window, the Lock, the sheet, and the dialogs on 2026-11-12 (`docs/human-checklist.md`).
- `screen-reader` reads each system's own tree (the Mac's AX tree, Windows' UI Automation control view, `tests/lib/windows.ts`): the sidebar's rows and their places, the shown tab, the edge's width, the Settings button, Settings' headings and a picker's choice, the App icon dialog and its chosen sky, the sheet's chosen color, the proxy's fields only once it is on and its switch on still once the pointer has left it, Quit Pane? and an extension's prompt by their titles, and the PIN's dots. `profile-sheet` reads a choice checked and the row named with its tabs on Windows.
- Narrator itself walked every surface in the test VM on 2026-11-16 (W8c), and W8d's changes on the installed Pane: the Lock, a wrong PIN, Change PIN's steps, the extensions menu, the edge's keys, and Exit Pane?'s Cancel by Enter.
- `pin-unsaved` reads, in each system's tree, the lines of a PIN the disk didn't save and its two buttons by their titles; in the test VM, W8c's listener heard a system alert from the window at each refused write, Try again's included (W8e).
- `screen-reader` also reads the PIN's field (`AXSecureTextField` named PIN on the Mac; an Edit named PIN, protected, its description the count, on Windows), Set up PIN's dialog by its steps and the Lock's, the edge's width after each arrow once a screen reader has focused it, and on Windows the prompt's Cancel given the keys (a real Tab in front, a screen reader's focus behind) and pressed by Return, cancelling it (Esc on the Mac); `sidebar-resize` steps the edge by its arrows, held at its limits.
