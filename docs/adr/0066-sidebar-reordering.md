# Profiles and tabs reorder as the Electron app's did, live and as one

Profiles, and each profile's tabs, reorder by a drag that behaves and animates as the Electron app's did, at the user's request: locked to the vertical axis, and never out of the sidebar. The Electron app used dnd-kit's sortable lists: a drag after 5 points, its vertical-axis modifier, the source at 40% in the list, and an overlay riding the pointer. `VerticalReorder` (`app/Pane/Views/Components/VerticalReorder.swift`) does the same in SwiftUI. The system drag (`.draggable`, `.dropDestination`, `DraggedItem`) is gone: its preview could leave the window and move sideways.

While dragging, the row stays in the list at 40% and moves to where it would drop, and the rows around it slide aside on a 0.28-second spring (`Motion.slide`). A copy of the header or tab rides the pointer on the card's fill with a soft shadow, its top held inside the list however far the pointer goes. On release, the copy settles onto the row's place, then the order is kept (`moveProfile`, `moveTab`). The drop lands at the place whose top is nearest the copy's, as dnd-kit's closest center, each top counted with the dragged row out of the list, so an expanded profile's whole height counts. That math is `Reorder` in PaneKit, with tests. A click that moves less than 5 points still toggles the profile.

The drag runs beside the row's press, so a row moves while it is dragged. The rows are buttons, and as the button's `gesture` the drag's moves waited for the press to end, at the release: holding and dragging did nothing, the user reported. Measured in a window of its own, eight moves all arrived at the release, 275 ms after the first. As a `highPriorityGesture` each landed within half a millisecond, but a click's press showed only at the release. As a `simultaneousGesture`, the form taken, the moves land as fast and the press shows in 3 ms. Across 120 moves, the row stands at its new place 0.25 ms after the move at the median, 0.56 ms at p90, and 3.6 ms at most. A release on the dragged row, which stands under the pointer by then, is no click: the buttons skip their action while a row is dragged or settling (`VerticalReorder.isDragging`). The dragged row shows no hover or press of its own (`isDraggedRow`).

A row moves as one, after the user's report that a sliding tab's favicon and title moved at different times. SwiftUI hands a layout move down to each icon and text, and one that an update redraws as it moves takes that update's animation. A row whose hover changed while it slid moved its title on the hover's 0.15-second ease and its favicon on the slide's spring, up to 72 px apart, measured in a window of its own. Every sidebar row (`RowButtonStyle`: tabs, profile headers, New tab, New, Settings) is a geometry group (`.geometryGroup()`), so the list animates its place and its contents keep theirs. The hover's fades and the profile bar's morph (ADR 0064) are unchanged.

`sidebar-reorder` drags through the event queue (`pointerWindow`): a profile dragged down two places, one dragged up and out of the window (it lands first), a short click, and a tab dragged to the end. It reads the rows' frames through accessibility before the release (`tests/tools/ax-frames.swift`). Before the fix they stood where the drag began; now the dragged row stands where it will drop and the others have slid aside, and a release on it toggles and selects nothing. The scenario's first note, that a window in the background takes the moves in only at the release, was this bug. No scenario shows a row moving as one: SwiftUI's hover follows the real pointer alone, and events posted to the window never move it, so the measurement drove the hover state itself.
