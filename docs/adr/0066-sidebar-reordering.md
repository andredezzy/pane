# Profiles and tabs reorder as the Electron app's did, live and as one

Profiles, and each profile's tabs, reorder by a drag that behaves and animates as the Electron app's did, at the user's request: locked to the vertical axis, and never out of the sidebar. The Electron app used dnd-kit's sortable lists: a drag after 5 points, its vertical-axis modifier, the source at 40% in the list, and an overlay riding the pointer. `VerticalReorder` (`pane/ui/sidebar/vertical_reorder.cc`) does the same, as the Swift app's did in SwiftUI, where the system drag (`.draggable`, `.dropDestination`) went first: its preview could leave the window and move sideways.

While dragging, the row stays in the list at 40% and moves to where it would drop, and the rows around it slide aside on a 0.28-second spring (`motion::Slide`), each on a layer of its own, moved by its transform, keeping its speed at each new target. A copy of the header or tab (`pane/ui/sidebar/drag_copy.cc`) rides the pointer on a layer of its own, on the card's fill with a soft shadow, the card's alone, as Paper 03.11 draws it, its top held inside the list however far the pointer goes. On release, the copy settles onto the row's place, then the order is kept (`UiApp::MoveProfile`, `MoveTab`). The drop lands at the place whose top is nearest the copy's, as dnd-kit's closest center, each top counted with the dragged row out of the list, so an expanded profile's whole height counts. That math is `model::ReorderTarget` (`pane/model/reorder.h`), with tests. A click that moves less than 5 points still toggles the profile.

**The drag runs beside the row's press,** so a row moves while it is dragged. `VerticalReorder` watches the rows' mouse events ahead of them, a pre-target handler on their list, and lets each through: a press shows at once, and a release on the dragged row, which stands under the pointer by then, is no click, since the rows skip their action while a row is dragged or settling (`is_dragging`). The dragged row shows no hover or press of its own. A drag step reaches the window's layout in 0.06 to 0.08 ms at the median (p90 0.16, max 0.32). In the Swift app the rows were buttons, and as the button's `gesture` the drag's moves waited for the press to end, at the release: holding and dragging did nothing, the user reported (measured in a window of its own, eight moves all arrived at the release, 275 ms after the first). As a `highPriorityGesture` each landed within half a millisecond, but a click's press showed only at the release; as a `simultaneousGesture`, the form taken, the moves landed as fast and the press showed in 3 ms, and the row stood at its new place 0.25 ms after the move at the median, 0.56 ms at p90, and 3.6 ms at most.

**A row moves as one,** after the user's report that a sliding tab's favicon and title moved at different times. In the Swift app SwiftUI handed a layout move down to each icon and text, and one that an update redrew as it moved took that update's animation: a row whose hover changed while it slid moved its title on the hover's 0.15-second ease and its favicon on the slide's spring, up to 72 px apart, until every sidebar row was a geometry group (`.geometryGroup()`). In Views a row slides as one layer, so it moves as one by construction. The hover's fades and the profile bar's morph (ADR 0064) are unchanged. The Swift app's copy also shadowed its title and icon onto the card, up to 4/255, where the Views copy's shadow is the card's alone.

`sidebar-reorder` drags through the event queue (`pointerWindow`, which hands each phase to the root view, ADR 0012): a profile dragged down two places, one dragged up and out of the window (it lands first), a short click, and a tab dragged to the end. It reads the rows' and the copy's frames before the release, through the channel (`frames`, ADR 0018). Before the fix they stood where the drag began; now the dragged row stands where it will drop and the others have slid aside, and a release on it toggles and selects nothing. The scenario's first note, that a window in the background takes the moves in only at the release, was this bug. `tests/rigs/views-reorder.ts` renders a drag's states, the rows mid-slide, and the sidebar after each drop beside the same order reached with no drag, which must match it to the pixel, and times a drag step over 120 moves.
