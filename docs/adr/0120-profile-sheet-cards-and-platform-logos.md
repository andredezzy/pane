# The profile sheet holds its settings in cards, and picks a fingerprint's platform by its real logo

The sheet had grown a field at a time from the Electron app's (ADR 0034): a column of switches and fields with no explanation of what each did. Adding Save history (ADR 0119) was the moment to lay it out again. Paper's 05.4 (New) and 05.5 (Edit) draw it, in light and dark, 05.6 to 05.11 and 03.7 the states those leave out, and 05.1 to 05.3, which drew the sheet before it, were redrawn to it. The user approved them on 2026-11-04, after asking for the platform's real logos ("um seletor com a logo real das plataformas") in place of a segmented control of words, and for more vertical padding on the tiles.

What it is (`pane::ProfileSheet`, `pane/ui/sheets/profile_sheet.h`, its form `model::ProfileDraft`), over the page at the window's right:

- **400 points wide**, from 360, for three tiles and a sentence under each switch, inset 8 from the window: the title and a close X, a body that scrolls between a rule under the header and one over the footer, then Cancel and Create (Save for an edit).
- **The name and the color**, at the top, with no card: labels at 12 on 16-point lines, fields at 13, swatches 8 apart. The sheet shows no Group (the user's call, 2026-11-11): a saved `group` stays in the data byte for byte, for an earlier Pane to read back.
- **Privacy, Fingerprint, and Network**, each a label over a card, as Settings' groups (ADR 0065) at the panel's scale: rounded 15, over a fill of its own. Each switch has a sentence under it that says what its state does, and changes with it, crossfading (`motion::Status`).
- **The platform:** three tiles 72 tall, each a logo over its name, the chosen one ringed in the foreground as the App icon picker's is (ADR 0089). Choosing is instant; hover fills the others.
- **Time zone,** the last row of the Fingerprint card while spoofing is on (Paper's 05.1 to 05.5, and 05.7 for its menu): Automatic, which follows the computer (ADR 0038), or a zone from the system's menu, by region, each with today's offset. A system menu rather than a searchable field: it is the app's one picker (`MenuButton`, ADR 0065), and it finds by typing. Its note names the computer's zone while Automatic. The zones are listed off the UI thread each time the sheet opens, so the first frame does not wait for them and a Pane left open across a clock change shows the day's offsets.
- **A warning** when the platform is not the computer's (`model::HostPlatform()`), on amber, which comes and goes with the layout's move (`motion::Toggle`), though the click that causes it lands at once.
- **One address field:** the proxy's type, host, and port, the type's menu holding the width of its longest option, so the host does not move when it changes. The separate paste field is gone. A proxy string pasted into the host fills the fields; typed there, it fills them when the field is left, or on Test connection or Save, since a host typed a key at a time must not lose its colons as they come. An IPv6 address, bracketed or not, is a host and never such a string: read as one, a rename's Save rewrote the host, the port, and the credentials. The port's segment holds 65535. With the switch on, the proxy does not save without a host or with one Chrome cannot read (a scheme, credentials, a path): the first saved as no proxy, under a sheet saying "Its traffic goes through this proxy", so the profile went direct, and the second failed its every page.
- **A problem is a red ring, with no words** (the user's call over the first draft's message, 2026-11-05): a field that keeps the sheet from saving takes a red ring in place of its border, the name's (Paper's 05.10), the host's cell in its dividers' place (05.8 and 05.9), and the port's at the field's rounded end (05.11). It replaces the focus ring while it shows, stays until the field can save, and VoiceOver hears why (ADR 0071). Save brings the field into view, since it can be past the body's end and nothing else would answer the press. The port's words stay beside Test connection, which has no field to ring when the test cannot even start.
- **The body scrolls between two rules, the header's and the footer's, in the border color in both themes.** They were white 5%, the Electron app's, which only showed on dark, so on light the proxy's card cut hard at nothing. 0.16.1 gave the body the sidebar's fade for that (ADR 0061). On seeing it beside the rules on dark, the user gave the rule: a fade is for an edge with nothing to mark it (DESIGN.md › Scroll edges). So the rules show on light too, and the body does not fade.
- **The proxy test beside its button**, a dot and words, not on the button: yellow "Testing…", emerald "Connected · 45.12.8.19", or the error on red (ADR 0037). An answer is dropped once the fields no longer hold the proxy it tested. The error reads "Connection refused" and "Connection timed out" as Paper words them, and otherwise Chrome's error name as a phrase ("Tunnel connection failed"), the same words on every OS (the user's call, 2026-11-11), where the Swift app showed the system's message.

The words the boards leave to the states: "Sites see this Mac." with spoofing off, and "Keeps no pages or downloads." on a profile with nothing to clear.

**The logos** are drawn from their own vector paths, at the boards' sizes (`pane/ui/sheets/platform_logo.cc`; `NOTICE` gives their origins): Windows' four squares at 22 points in its #0078D4, Apple's at 26, in the foreground's one color, and Tux at 26, with a faint edge on dark, where the black back would melt away. Tux's soft shadow is left out: it does not belong at 26 points. The Swift app drew them from vectors in its asset catalog.

The switch stays the app's one (ADR 0070): the boards' first draft had drawn a lighter one by hand, and they were corrected to Paper's control.

Rejected:

- **A segmented control of words,** the first draft: the user asked for the logos.
- **One-colour logos** from one icon set, so they would sit with Lucide's: these are other companies' marks, and the point is to recognise them at once.
- **A wider card of switches with the fields beside them:** Settings' layout at 640 points, which a side panel has no room for.
- **A message under a field that can't save,** the first draft's: the user asked for the ring alone.

**Its first frame.** The sheet opens in 3.8 ms at the median (p90 9.1) on the Views build, against the Swift app's 38.0; choosing a platform takes 7.05 ms at the median, about 2 of them work, the rest Chromium's next frame (2026-11-11). The Swift app's had come down from 0.17.0's p90 of 75 to 88 ms with the name field's focus after that frame, the form set as the sheet was made, and the zones off the main thread, what was left being SwiftUI's layout and AppKit's drag margins for a full-size content view.

Checked by `tests/scenarios/profile-sheet.ts`, which fills the sheet as a user does, through its fields and menus, reads its accessibility, and renders each state in both themes, and by `tests/rigs/views-profile-sheet.ts`, which renders the states the scenario leaves out against Paper 05.5, 05.6, and 05.10. Against the Swift app's renders the sheet differs on 2.0 to 3.4% of its pixels, the text's accepted weight and height (ADR 0060) and what follows from them. `ProfileDraftTest` checks the form, a saved group kept among it (`GroupKept`).
