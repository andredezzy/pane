# Controllers are split by what they own, and the model lives in PaneKit

The app's controllers are split by what each one owns, and the browser's state, `BrowserModel`, lives in PaneKit, where it knows no engine and no file, so its rules run in tests. The split came from the architecture review of 2026-10-28 (its arch track's A1, A2, A7, and P4), fixed under the user's standing order to fix everything found. Behavior stayed as it was, and every scenario (59) passed on the final build, against the frozen engine, `complete` in each report.

The controller is split by what it owns (A1): `app/Pane/Browser/BrowserController.swift` went from 1,253 lines to 945, beside `+Intents` (136) and `+Actions` (152). `ExtensionsController` owns each profile's extension list (out of `BrowserModel`, which keeps no `NSImage` or `NSColor`), the puzzle's menu, the more menu, the add and remove popups and their Report abuse box, the toolbar's anchors, the Extensions page's anchor, and the action popover. Seven fields left `WindowState`, and the extension views take it, not the browser controller. `PageRegistry` holds each loaded tab's `WebContents` both ways, its docked DevTools, and the tab an inserted page takes (`opening(_:)`, in place of a `pendingTabID` set and cleared around the call); the controller still decides when a page comes and goes.

Views take what they use where that is clean: Settings takes the settings, the lock, the updater, and the change it makes; the profile sheet the profile and its save; the extension views their controller. The root, the sidebar, the toolbar, and find keep the controller, for its intents (ADR 0078). Of the 10 view types that took the whole controller, 7 still do, all intent callers. The feature has its folder (A7): `app/Pane/Extensions/` holds the controller, the popover, the model, the menu, the popups, the page, and the actions, and `VerticalReorder` and `WindowDragArea` joined the view layer's `app/Pane/Views/Components/` (ADR 0070).

`BrowserModel` (A2) takes its timestamps and its 300 ms save wait from a clock (`BrowserModel.Clock`), and each save hands a copy of what changed to a `persist` closure. `ModelStore` (`app/Pane/Model/ModelStore.swift`), in the app, reads `profiles.json`, `settings.json`, and the Keychain's proxy passwords (ADR 0069), and writes on its utility queue, as the safety track made it (ADR 0068): the main thread still only copies. A settings change that changes nothing writes nothing (the sidebar's reset to its width, say). PaneKit's 14 model tests cover insertion after an anchor, removal into the closed tabs, a deleted profile's tabs, selection and its MRU, the selection's observers, clamped moves (a negative destination no longer traps), an edit's proxy and fingerprint changes, the clock's stamps, an unloaded tab's state, and the debounced save (one write per burst, only the changed file, nothing for an unchanged title, `saveNow` at once). Two rules went to PaneKit with the model, each with tests: `ExtensionPromptText` (Add … to Pane?, curled quotes, Report abuse's URL; ADR 0056) and `CrashLoop` (a second crash within a minute shows the sad tab; ADR 0027).

Observation granularity was measured, and left as it is (P4). The toolbar reads every tab's loading progress, so a background tab's load can run its body. Counted with each body logging itself, 20 background reloads across 5 tabs ran the toolbar's body 8 times, as many as 4 reloads of the selected tab did: SwiftUI coalesces the changes per frame. Per-tab observable objects would save nothing visible. The sidebar's sections and rows, which did run for changes they do not show, take values instead (ADR 0073).
